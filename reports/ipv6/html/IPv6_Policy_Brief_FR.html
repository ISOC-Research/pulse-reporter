Executive summary

France currently demonstrates a leading position in national IPv6 adoption, with 86.0% of its internet users capable of accessing content over IPv6. This places the country at a strong global rank of #4, significantly above the global average. A key strength is the universal compliance of major Internet Service Providers (ISPs), indicating widespread network readiness at the access layer. Despite this impressive user-side deployment, a structural paradox exists where operational configuration and content readiness lag behind network capability. Specifically, there is effectively zero IPv6 readiness among the nation's popular web domains (0.0%), and government websites show only a modest 22.92% readiness, highlighting a significant disconnect between user access and content availability over the modern internet protocol.

Further analysis reveals critical areas for improvement in routing security and DNS infrastructure. While overall RPKI coverage for IPv6 prefixes is high at 97.87%, several networks still exhibit weak adoption, posing potential security vulnerabilities. Domestic Internet Exchange Points (IXPs) are largely IPv6-ready (96.3% with IPv6 LANs), but a segment of their members (9.3%) has yet to enable IPv6 peering, indicating room for optimisation. The national .fr ccTLD shows 39.50% AAAA record readiness, which is lower than comparable TLDs, and authoritative nameserver IPv6 reachability stands at 81.14%. Critically, only 36.67% of glue records are IPv6-capable, suggesting a fragility in core DNS infrastructure that could affect resilience and reachability. Addressing these gaps in RPKI, web content, and DNS will be essential to fully leverage France's strong IPv6 user adoption and secure its digital ecosystem.

1. Where France stands

France exhibits a formidable position in global IPv6 adoption, with 86.0% of its internet users operating over the protocol. This places the nation as the 4th highest globally out of 243 countries, significantly surpassing the global average of 24.4%. This high level of adoption is largely driven by ubiquitous IPv6 deployment among major Internet Service Providers, with 0.0% of the subscriber market served by non-compliant ISPs. Such widespread enablement positions France as a leader in consumer-facing IPv6 readiness, allowing its citizens to benefit from the modern internet protocol. The closest peer country by adoption score, UM (United States Minor Outlying Islands), stands at 88.31%, indicating that France is at the vanguard of national IPv6 transition efforts.

Two readings matter for policy. First, the exceptionally high national IPv6 adoption rate underscores successful ISP-led deployment initiatives and highlights France's commitment to modern internet infrastructure. The full compliance of major ISPs indicates that foundational access to IPv6 is largely resolved, shifting the focus towards leveraging this widespread capability. Second, despite the strong user adoption, significant vulnerabilities remain in the national digital ecosystem's security posture and content availability over IPv6, particularly concerning routing security (RPKI), popular web content, government services, and fundamental DNS infrastructure. These areas represent critical next steps to ensure the security, resilience, and full utility of France's IPv6-enabled internet.

2.2 Strong IXP IPv6 Readiness

France's Internet Exchange Point (IXP) ecosystem demonstrates strong readiness for IPv6, with 96.3% of the 27 domestic IXPs providing an IPv6 LAN. This high rate indicates that the foundational infrastructure for IPv6 peering is largely in place across the country's interconnection points. The widespread availability of IPv6 peering facilities at IXPs is crucial for fostering local traffic exchange and reducing reliance on international transit for IPv6 content.

Furthermore, 90.7% of IXP members (1334 out of 1471) have already enabled IPv6 peering. This metric signifies a high level of operational engagement with IPv6 at the peering layer, contributing to more efficient and direct IPv6 routing paths within France. Major IXPs such as France-IX Paris, Equinix Paris, and nine exhibit strong IPv6 member participation, with many approaching or achieving full IPv6 peering among their members, such as BGP.Exchange - Lyon, BreizhIX, and AuvernIX, which report 100% IPv6 member participation.

3. Where France could improve

France could significantly enhance its digital infrastructure resilience by addressing weaknesses in RPKI coverage, web content readiness, and authoritative DNS IPv6 deployment.

3.1 Improving RPKI Coverage for IPv6 Routes

Despite a high overall RPKI coverage of 97.87% for IPv6 routing prefixes in France, which indicates a strong commitment to routing security, a closer examination reveals that certain operators still fall short of full compliance. The remaining 2.13% of unprotected prefixes represent potential vulnerabilities that could be exploited for route hijacks or other routing incidents. While the overall figure is positive, ensuring comprehensive RPKI adoption across all networks is critical for maintaining the integrity and trustworthiness of the national routing table.

Several ISPs, despite their significant IPv6 routing presence, continue to exhibit weaker RPKI coverage compared to the national average. For instance, ALPHALINK-AS Alphalink SASU, FLEXNETWORK-AS FLEX NETWORK SARL, and BANDWIDTH-AS Hydra Communications Ltd show RPKI coverage below 90%. Addressing these specific gaps through targeted outreach and technical assistance is necessary to achieve near-100% RPKI coverage, thereby fortifying France's IPv6 routing infrastructure against common security threats.

3.3 Increasing ccTLD and DNS Infrastructure IPv6 Adoption

The national .fr ccTLD exhibits a moderate IPv6 AAAA readiness of 39.50% based on sampled domains. While not critically low, this figure falls below that of the global .com TLD (50.28%) and is comparable to .de (40.10%) and .in (42.18%). This suggests that a significant portion of domains under the .fr ccTLD have yet to enable IPv6 connectivity, potentially limiting the reachability and efficiency of access to French-specific online services for IPv6-only users in the future.

Furthermore, a notable vulnerability lies in the IPv6 readiness of glue records, with only 36.67% of assessed glue-style nameservers being IPv6-capable. Glue records are essential for resolving domain names within their own zone and their limited IPv6 enablement poses a foundational weakness in the resilience of the .fr DNS infrastructure. Although authoritative nameserver IPv6 reachability is higher at 81.14%, the low glue record readiness indicates that self-hosted authoritative DNS infrastructure specifically requires focused attention to improve overall IPv6 health and resilience for the national ccTLD.

6.2 Loopholes to avoid creating

  • Avoid mandating specific IPv6 implementation technologies or vendor solutions, preserving market competition and operator flexibility.
  • Refrain from imposing arbitrary IPv6 transition deadlines without adequate capacity building, technical assistance, and financial support for affected stakeholders.
  • Do not prematurely mandate IPv6-only environments for all services, as a gradual dual-stack transition is generally more practical and less disruptive.
  • Prevent the creation of policies that disproportionately increase administrative or financial burdens on small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs) during the IPv6 transition.

Conclusion

France stands at a unique juncture in its IPv6 transition, characterised by exceptionally high user adoption rates, demonstrating robust network-side deployment by its Internet Service Providers. This success in enabling IPv6 access for the majority of its citizens forms a strong foundation for a more resilient and future-proof internet. However, a significant disconnect persists between this widespread user capability and the operational readiness of web content, government services, and critical DNS infrastructure. The negligible IPv6 readiness of popular web domains and the suboptimal adoption within the public sector represent key challenges that prevent the full realisation of IPv6's benefits.

Addressing these specific gaps through a staged approach, starting with no-capital configuration changes and administrative coordination, is paramount. Prioritising quick wins such as targeted RPKI outreach, promoting AAAA record deployment, and launching government IPv6 readiness programs can rapidly enhance security and content availability. By sequencing these initiatives before embarking on more capital-heavy projects, France can build regulatory momentum, demonstrate tangible progress, and progressively align its content and security posture with its impressive user-side IPv6 adoption, ensuring a more resilient and robust internet for all its citizens.
