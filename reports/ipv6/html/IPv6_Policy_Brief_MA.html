The values presented in this document are the Internet Resilience Index, normalized to 1–100. These values should be interpreted as a score rather than an absolute value. In some cases, the raw absolute values are presented to provide additional context.

Executive summary

Morocco currently exhibits a national IPv6 adoption rate of 3.8%, positioning the country significantly below the global average of 24.4%. While preliminary graph-based analysis indicates that 100% of assessed domestic Internet Service Providers (ISPs) possess IPv6-capable upstream reachability, there is a stark disconnect between transit capability and end-user delivery. This structural paradox—where the core is ready but the edge remains stagnant—leaves 99.9% of the national subscriber market served by ISPs classified as non-compliant or laggards in IPv6 transition.

The deployment landscape is defined by extreme market concentration. Two bottleneck providers, Office National des Postes et Telecommunications (IAM) and MEDITELECOM, together control over 95% of the market but maintain adoption rates below 4.1%. Security metrics further highlight operational gaps; national RPKI coverage for IPv6 prefixes stands at 34.17%, primarily due to a total absence of Route Origin Authorizations (ROAs) within the dominant provider's network. Furthermore, domestic interconnection infrastructure via CAS-IX and FEZIX lacks IPv6 LAN support, and 0% of popular domestic web domains are reachable via IPv6, indicating a total reliance on legacy IPv4 infrastructure for local digital services.

1. Where Morocco stands

Morocco ranks 187th globally in IPv6 adoption, trailing behind regional peers and international benchmarks. With a score of 3.8%, the country sits nearly 21 percentage points below the global average. The growth trajectory has been largely stagnant, moving from 1.0% in 2020 to 3.8% in late 2026. This sluggish performance suggests that while the technical foundations are present in the core networks, there is a lack of regulatory or commercial momentum to push deployment to the last mile.

Two readings matter for policy. First, the 100% upstream reachability indicates that there are no external technical barriers to IPv6; the bottleneck is entirely internal and likely related to Customer Premises Equipment (CPE) or internal network configurations. Second, the heavy market concentration means that policy interventions targeting just two Autonomous Systems (IAM and MEDITELECOM) would resolve over 90% of the national adoption deficit.

2. Where Morocco is improving

2.1 Incremental ccTLD modernization

The .ma ccTLD ecosystem is showing signs of modernization, particularly in the DNS layer. Currently, 39.9% of sampled .ma domains resolve to IPv6-capable infrastructure, an increase of 2.8 percentage points since mid-2026. This growth indicates that hosting providers and registrars are increasingly enabling dual-stack configurations, even if the underlying end-user ISPs have not yet enabled the protocol for subscribers.

2.2 Targeted security compliance among mid-tier providers

While national RPKI coverage is low, select providers have achieved full compliance. MEDITELECOM (AS36925) and CNRST (AS30983) maintain 100% RPKI coverage for their IPv6 prefixes. This demonstrates that the local technical community possesses the necessary expertise to implement routing security; the challenge is scaling these best practices to the dominant provider, IAM, which currently hosts 78 prefixes with 0% coverage.

3. Where Morocco could improve

The current state of IPv6 in Morocco is undermined by three primary bottlenecks: extreme ISP inertia, absent local interconnection support, and a lack of DNS glue record modernization.

3.1 High-impact ISP bottlenecks

The national adoption rate is severely suppressed by two major operators. Office National des Postes et Telecommunications (IAM) holds a 62.7% market share but has only achieved 4.0% adoption. MEDITELECOM, with 33.1% market share, sits at 3.6% adoption. Both networks have had IPv6 allocations for over a decade, indicating that the delay is not due to lack of resources but rather operational prioritization.

3.2 Infrastructure and interconnection gaps

Domestic Internet Exchange Points (IXPs) are not currently facilitating IPv6 traffic exchange. Neither CAS-IX nor FEZIX provides an IPv6 LAN for peering. While some members at CAS-IX are IPv6-capable, the lack of a native IPv6 fabric at the exchange forces domestic traffic to either remain on IPv4 or trombone through international transit points, increasing latency and costs.

3.3 Web services and DNS health

Reachability for popular domestic websites remains at 0%, meaning no major Moroccan commercial site is natively accessible over IPv6. Furthermore, 0% of glue-style nameservers for the .ma TLD are IPv6-capable. This creates a circular dependency where IPv6-only users (globally) may struggle to resolve Moroccan domains if their DNS resolvers cannot reach .ma nameservers over IPv6.

6. Policy gaps and loopholes

6.1 Where policy is absent or unenforced

Gap Consequence Remedy
Absence of CPE standards ISPs continue importing IPv4-only or locked hardware. Set minimum IPv6 certification for imported routers.
No IXP funding for IPv6 Local exchanges remain legacy-only, increasing transit costs. Subsidy for IXP IPv6-only peering fabrics.
Stagnant government readiness Public sector fails to lead by example. Mandatory IPv6 in all digital government tenders.
Lack of RPKI enforcement High risk of BGP hijacking for IPv6 prefixes. ANRT requirement for ROAs on all critical prefixes.

6.2 Loopholes to avoid creating

  • Allowing "carrier-grade NAT" (CGNAT) investment to be used as a justification for delaying IPv6 deployment.
  • Permitting ISPs to claim IPv6 readiness based solely on core-network BGP announcements without last-mile user enablement.
  • Exempting small or regional ISPs from IPv6 mandates, which creates a multi-speed digital economy.
  • Ignoring the DNS layer (Glue records) while focusing only on traffic, which leaves infrastructure brittle.

Conclusion

Morocco faces a critical disconnect between its core network capabilities and operational reality. While the country's ISPs have successfully secured IPv6 transit and maintained active upstream reachability, the failure to transition the last mile and secure the routing layer with RPKI has resulted in one of the lowest adoption rates in the region. The concentration of the market within two primary operators presents a unique opportunity: targeted regulatory pressure on these entities could single-handedly elevate Morocco to a regional leader.

The path forward requires sequencing cheap configuration wins, such as enabling RPKI and updating DNS glue records, before embarking on capital-heavy projects. By mandating IPv6 in public sector procurement and auditing ISP CPE stocks, the regulator can build the necessary momentum to ensure that Morocco's digital infrastructure is resilient, secure, and ready for the global transition away from legacy IPv4.
