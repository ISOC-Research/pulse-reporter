Strategic Synthesis & Roadmap for Digital Future

Sir/Ma'am,

This report synthesizes our findings on the nation's IPv6 adoption status, outlining critical vulnerabilities and presenting a strategic roadmap for securing our digital future. The implications of our current posture extend far beyond mere technical metrics, directly impacting our national security, economic competitiveness, and geopolitical standing.

Urgent Alert: Our nation currently operates with a critically low IPv6 adoption rate of 0.5%. This exposes us to significant cybersecurity risks, hampers economic growth, and isolates us from the evolving global internet.

Executive Summary: The "Big Picture" Diagnosis

The core narrative emerging from our analysis is a striking paradox: We possess the fundamental components for global internet connectivity, with 100% IPv6-capable upstream reachability for our ISPs, yet our national IPv6 adoption rate stands at a mere 0.5%. This indicates a profound disconnect between capability and deployment, primarily driven by internal structural inefficiencies and a lack of regulatory enforcement.

The entirety of our subscriber market (100%) is served by ISPs classified as non-compliant, with two major bottleneck providers (Hormuud Telecom and Telesom) alone accounting for over 61% of the market share. Furthermore, a staggering 11 ISPs, representing 36.2% of the market, lack even basic IPv6 prefix allocations. This translates into widespread routing insecurity, demonstrated by an RPKI coverage of only 26.32%, leaving our national digital infrastructure vulnerable to hijacking and foreign interference.

While there are nascent efforts in government web readiness (75% on a limited sample), critical sectors like Banking, News, Education, and Ecommerce show 0% IPv6 readiness. This digital vulnerability is not just a technical oversight; it's a strategic liability that can stifle innovation, deter foreign investment, and compromise our national data integrity. Addressing this gap is paramount for our digital sovereignty and future prosperity.

SWOT Analysis: The Strategic Cheat Sheet

💪 Strengths

  • High Upstream Readiness: 100% of assessed ISPs maintain IPv6-capable upstream connectivity, suggesting external network readiness to support transition.
  • Government Initiative (Nascent): Limited government domains show 75% IPv6 readiness, indicating a potential willingness to lead by example, if scaled.
  • Foundational TLD Health: Our .so ccTLD exhibits 51.09% IPv6 readiness with 80.87% NS IPv6 reachability, providing a base for digital identity.

⚠️ Weaknesses

  • Critically Low National Adoption: A mere 0.5% IPv6 adoption places us among the lowest globally, creating digital isolation.
  • Dominant Non-Compliance: 100% of the market is served by non-compliant ISPs, with key players acting as significant bottlenecks.
  • Severe Routing Insecurity: Only 26.32% RPKI coverage leaves our routing vulnerable to BGP hijacks and malicious redirection.
  • Lagging Digital Economy: Critical sectors (Banking, News, Education, Ecommerce) show 0% IPv6 readiness, hindering modernization and security.
  • Underutilized IXP: Despite a 100% IPv6-enabled IXP LAN, zero members are actively peering over IPv6.

🚀 Opportunities

  • Digital Economy Catalyst: IPv6 adoption is foundational for emerging technologies (IoT, AI, Smart Cities), unlocking new economic sectors and innovation.
  • Enhanced Cybersecurity: A robust IPv6 deployment, coupled with RPKI, will significantly bolster our national cybersecurity posture and resilience.
  • Regional Leadership: Proactive IPv6 adoption can position the nation as a leader in digital infrastructure, attracting investment and fostering collaboration.
  • Strategic Engagement: Targeted intervention with bottleneck ISPs offers an immediate and high-impact pathway to significant national adoption gains.

🛡️ Threats

  • Digital Isolation: Increasing global reliance on IPv6 will render our IPv4-centric infrastructure isolated, impacting connectivity and international digital trade.
  • Heightened Cyber Risk: Our low RPKI coverage and reliance on outdated protocols make us a prime target for BGP hijacking and cyber espionage.
  • Economic Stagnation: Inability to participate in the modern internet will stifle innovation, deter foreign direct investment, and limit economic diversification.
  • Loss of Digital Sovereignty: Continued reliance on IPv4 scarcity and foreign transit may lead to external control over our digital infrastructure and data flows.
  • Geopolitical Vulnerability: An insecure and outdated internet infrastructure can be exploited by state or non-state actors, compromising national security.

Strategic Roadmap: The Policy Agenda

Phase 1 — Quick Wins
  1. Emergency Regulatory Mandate for ISPs: Issue a decree requiring all ISPs to obtain IPv6 allocations from RIRs and announce prefixes within 90 days. Prioritize the 11 "Ghost" ISPs (36.2% market share) identified in the report.
  2. Direct Engagement with Bottleneck Providers: Initiate high-level government dialogue with Hormuud Telecom (42.9% market share) and Telesom (18.4% market share). Establish clear, aggressive IPv6 deployment targets and timelines for these critical providers.
  3. Mandatory RPKI Enforcement: Issue a regulatory requirement for all ISPs to implement Route Origin Authorizations (ROAs) for all announced IPv6 prefixes, immediately improving our routing security from 26.32%.
  4. Last-Mile Equipment Standards: Introduce mandatory import standards for Customer Premises Equipment (CPE) and routers, ensuring all new devices are IPv6-compatible to address "Laggard" ISP issues.
  5. IXP IPv6 Peering Mandate: Require all current and future IXP members to enable IPv6 peering sessions within 60 days to activate the existing 100% IPv6-capable IXP LAN.
Phase 2 — Structural
  1. Sectoral IPv6 Adoption Targets: Develop and enforce specific IPv6 adoption targets for critical sectors (e.g., Banking, News, Education, E-commerce) through ministerial directives, starting with pilot programs.
  2. Public Sector IPv6 Transformation: Mandate 100% IPv6 readiness for all government websites and digital services by 2027, leveraging existing expertise and successes (e.g., the 75% readiness on assessed government domains).
  3. Infrastructure Modernization Program: Launch a national program offering incentives or subsidies for ISPs and enterprises to upgrade their network infrastructure and hardware to be IPv6-native.
  4. National Capacity Building: Establish a robust training and certification program for network engineers and IT professionals across both public and private sectors, focusing on IPv6 deployment and security.
  5. Digital Infrastructure Audit: Conduct a comprehensive audit of all national critical digital infrastructure to identify and remediate all IPv4-only dependencies.
Phase 3 — Vision
  1. National Digital Sovereignty Framework: Develop a comprehensive long-term strategy for digital independence, ensuring our internet infrastructure is resilient, secure, and under national control.
  2. Regional Leadership in Digital Connectivity: Position the nation as a leader in IPv6 adoption and internet security within the region, fostering cross-border collaboration and knowledge sharing.
  3. Innovation Ecosystem for IPv6-Native Services: Create an enabling environment, including incubators and research grants, to foster the development of IPv6-native applications, IoT solutions, and smart city initiatives.
  4. Continuous Monitoring and Adaptation: Establish a permanent, inter-ministerial IPv6 Task Force to continuously monitor adoption rates, identify new challenges, and adapt national policies to global internet trends.
  5. Digital Literacy and Awareness Campaigns: Launch public campaigns to educate citizens and businesses about the benefits of IPv6, driving demand for modern internet services.
