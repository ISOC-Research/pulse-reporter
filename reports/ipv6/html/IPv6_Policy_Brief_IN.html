The values presented in this document are the Internet Resilience Index, normalized to 1-100. These values should be interpreted as a score rather than an absolute value. In some cases, the raw absolute values are presented to provide additional context.

Executive summary

India maintains a globally leading position in IPv6 deployment, ranking 5th out of 243 countries analyzed. With a national adoption rate of 78.9%, India exceeds the global average of 23.6% by a significant margin of 55.3 percentage points. This achievement is primarily driven by massive infrastructure modernization within the mobile telecommunications sector, where the two largest providers, Reliance Jio and Bharti Airtel, have successfully migrated the vast majority of their combined subscriber base to IPv6-capable networks. This high level of adoption establishes India as a model for large-scale network transition in emerging digital economies.

However, the data reveals a critical structural paradox: while user-side adoption is exceptionally high, routing security and service-side readiness lag significantly behind. RPKI coverage is notably low at 28.66%, leaving 18,428 IPv6 prefixes vulnerable to route hijacking or misconfiguration. Furthermore, while the private sector (specifically banking and news) shows readiness levels above 53%, the public sector remains a bottleneck with only 13.2% of government domains (.gov.in) supporting IPv6. This disconnect between network capability and service-layer availability limits the end-to-end benefits of the IPv6 protocol for secure, localized traffic exchange.

1. Where India stands

India's IPv6 adoption profile is characterized by its scale and the speed of its transition over the last decade. Nationally, adoption stands at 78.9%, positioning India among an elite group of countries—including France (83.09%) and Saint Helena (83.3%)—that have moved beyond the transition threshold. This performance places India well ahead of global and regional norms, largely due to the "greenfield" nature of its 4G/5G deployments which prioritized IPv6-only or dual-stack architectures from inception.

Two readings matter for policy. First, the infrastructure capability is already present; 99.56% of assessed ISPs maintain upstream dependencies that are IPv6-capable, suggesting that the core backbone is fully prepared. Second, the remaining 21.1% of non-adoption is likely concentrated in legacy fixed-line segments and smaller regional ISPs that have not yet refreshed Customer Premise Equipment (CPE). The focus of national policy must now shift from "deployment" to "optimization and security."

2. Where India is improving

2.1 Dominant ISP Compliance

The top tier of the Indian ISP market is almost entirely compliant with IPv6 standards. Reliance Jio, holding 46.3% of the market, maintains a 77.8% adoption rate. Bharti Airtel, across its mobility and broadband divisions (30.3% combined share), averages over 65% adoption. These operators have demonstrated long-term commitment, with BSNL and Reliance Jio having maintained IPv6 prefixes for over 13 years.

2.2 Peering and Interconnection

Domestic Internet Exchange Points (IXPs) show high readiness. Out of 40 domestic IXPs, 34 (85%) have established IPv6 LANs. Participation is also robust; at NIXI Mumbai and Extreme IX Bangalore, IPv6 peering participation exceeds 82% and 89% respectively. This ensures that IPv6 traffic can remain local, reducing latency and transit costs for the national ecosystem.

3. Where India could improve

The primary weaknesses in India’s Internet resilience relate to routing security, public-sector digital readiness, and DNS configuration, which collectively undermine the stability of the high-adoption network.

3.1 Critical Routing Security Gaps (RPKI)

Despite the high volume of IPv6 prefixes (25,831), only 28.66% are protected by RPKI Route Origin Authorizations (ROAs). This is a significant vulnerability. Large operators such as Vodafone Idea (VIL) and Tata Play Broadband have 0.0% RPKI coverage despite announcing hundreds of prefixes. This lack of cryptographic signing makes Indian traffic susceptible to BGP hijacking.

3.2 Government and Public Sector Lag

There is a stark contrast between private sector readiness and government services. While Banking (53.85%) and News (53.97%) sectors have made significant strides, only 13.2% of the 947 government domains assessed are reachable over IPv6. This forces 78.9% of IPv6-enabled citizens to use translation mechanisms (NAT64) to access essential public services, introducing unnecessary latency and failure points.

3.3 DNS Infrastructure and Glue Records

The .in ccTLD ecosystem exhibits signs of "partial transition." While 55.04% of authoritative nameservers are reachable via IPv6, the presence of IPv6 glue records—necessary for a complete IPv6-only resolution path—is effectively zero at 0.74%. This indicates that even when a website is IPv6-enabled, the DNS resolution process often still requires IPv4, creating a "hidden" dependency.

6.2 Loopholes to avoid creating

  • Mandating adoption without security: Pushing IPv6 volume without RPKI enforcement increases the "blast radius" of routing incidents.
  • Carrier-Grade NAT (CGNAT) Reliance: Over-investment in IPv4 preservation (CGNAT) by smaller ISPs may delay native IPv6 deployments.
  • Partial TLD Migration: Enabling IPv6 for nameservers without glue records creates a false sense of readiness that fails under IPv6-only testing.

Conclusion

India’s IPv6 status is a tale of two layers: a world-class network layer and an underdeveloped service and security layer. The achievement of nearly 80% user adoption is a testament to the success of market-led infrastructure competition. However, this massive footprint remains unnecessarily vulnerable due to the stagnation of RPKI signing and the slow pace of IPv6 adoption within the public sector digital ecosystem. The national resilience profile is currently weighted toward "access" rather than "security."

By sequencing low-cost configuration wins—such as RPKI signing for major laggards and the modernization of .in glue records—India can rapidly align its routing security with its adoption volume. These administrative improvements, combined with a targeted mandate for government digital services, will build the regulatory momentum necessary to tackle the more capital-heavy challenges of legacy CPE replacement in the fixed-line sector.
