The values presented in this document are the Internet Resilience Index, normalized to 1–100. These values should be interpreted as a score rather than an absolute value. In some cases, the raw absolute values are presented to provide additional context.

Executive summary

India has achieved a commendable national IPv6 adoption rate of 78.9%, securing a global rank of #5 among 243 countries. This positions India significantly above the global average of 23.6%, demonstrating a robust commitment to next-generation internet protocol deployment at the user level. The underlying infrastructure also reflects considerable progress, with 85.0% of domestic Internet Exchange Points (IXPs) offering IPv6 LANs and 77.3% of IXP members having configured IPv6 peering. Furthermore, an impressive 99.56% of assessed Internet Service Providers (ISPs) demonstrate IPv6-capable upstream reachability, indicating broad foundational readiness across the national network ecosystem. Despite these achievements, a critical paradox emerges where advanced user adoption is not consistently matched by security and operational best practices across all segments of the digital infrastructure.

Key areas requiring focused intervention include routing security and web service readiness. IPv6 RPKI (Resource Public Key Infrastructure) coverage stands at a suboptimal 28.66%, exposing a significant portion of IPv6 routing prefixes to potential hijacking or misdirection. Similarly, IPv6 readiness within government domains is alarmingly low at 13.20%, suggesting a disconnect between public sector digital transformation initiatives and core internet protocol upgrades. The health of the `.in` country code Top-Level Domain (ccTLD) infrastructure also presents vulnerabilities, with authoritative nameserver IPv6 reachability at 55.04% and glue record IPv6 readiness at a mere 0.74%. While only 0.2% of the subscriber market is currently served by non-compliant ISPs, one identified "Ghost" ISP (APSFL-AS), serving 0.2% of the market, lacks any IPv6 prefixes in the global routing table, necessitating immediate regulatory attention to ensure full market coverage and prevent future service fragmentation. Addressing these specific operational and security gaps will be crucial for India to fully leverage its strong user-side IPv6 momentum and enhance overall internet resilience.

1. Where India stands

India's national IPv6 adoption rate of 78.9% places it among the global leaders in internet protocol transition. This figure is exceptionally high, significantly surpassing the current global average of 23.6% by over 55 percentage points. This positions India at the #5 rank worldwide, indicating a highly successful large-scale deployment to end-users, largely driven by major service providers. The country's performance in this domain is comparable to or exceeds that of other advanced digital economies, demonstrating a strong capability in rolling out next-generation internet infrastructure.

The country's closest peers in terms of adoption include Falkland Islands (79.85%), Pitcairn Islands (77.78%), and Åland Islands (74.89%), while it lags slightly behind France (83.09%) and Saint Helena (83.3%). This clustering indicates that India is operating at the forefront of global IPv6 deployment, a testament to coordinated efforts within its telecommunications sector. However, the high user adoption presents a unique challenge: ensuring that the underlying web services, routing security, and public sector digital infrastructure keep pace with this widespread user capability to fully realise the benefits of IPv6.

Two readings matter for policy. First, the extraordinary user-side adoption rate provides a strong foundation and market incentive for all stakeholders to complete their IPv6 transitions. This widespread user capability means that a significant portion of the internet traffic originates from or terminates on IPv6-enabled devices, creating an imperative for content providers, government services, and infrastructure operators to ensure full IPv6 readiness. Second, despite this impressive user adoption, the disparities in RPKI coverage, government domain readiness, and ccTLD infrastructure health suggest that the policy focus must now shift from initial deployment to comprehensive security, operational integrity, and public service enablement to truly future-proof India's internet ecosystem.

2. Where India is improving

2.1 High User-Side IPv6 Adoption

India has achieved a remarkable level of IPv6 adoption among its internet users, with 78.9% of the national user base operating on the newer protocol. This places India in a leading global position, ranked #5 worldwide, significantly exceeding the global average. This widespread deployment to end-users has been a critical factor in future-proofing the national internet infrastructure and facilitating growth amidst IPv4 address exhaustion. The scale of this achievement reflects the successful efforts by major internet service providers to transition their subscriber bases.

The consistent growth in user adoption has created a robust environment for IPv6-only services and content delivery, alleviating the pressures associated with network address translation (NAT) and improving overall network efficiency. The country's performance demonstrates a sustained commitment to modern internet standards, positioning it as a benchmark for other developing and developed nations seeking to accelerate their own IPv6 transitions. Maintaining this momentum and ensuring corresponding readiness across all digital services will be key to capitalising on this foundational strength.

2.2 Robust IPv6 Presence at Internet Exchange Points

The national internet exchange point (IXP) ecosystem in India exhibits strong support for IPv6, with 85.0% of the 40 domestic IXPs operating an IPv6 LAN. This foundational readiness is crucial for enabling local IPv6 peering and reducing reliance on international transit for IPv6 traffic. Furthermore, 77.3% of the 1,895 members across these IXPs have configured IPv6 peering, indicating a significant commitment from network operators to exchange traffic natively over IPv6 within the country.

This high level of IPv6 enablement at IXPs ensures that local traffic can efficiently traverse the domestic internet, benefiting from lower latency and improved resilience. Major IXPs such as DE-CIX Mumbai, Extreme IX Mumbai, and NIXI Mumbai show strong IPv6 member participation rates, ranging from 70.2% to 88.1% across various key cities. Continued engagement with the remaining IXPs and their members will further solidify India's local peering infrastructure for IPv6 traffic.

2.3 Widespread ISP IPv6 Capability and Upstream Reachability

An extensive analysis of India's ISP landscape indicates that 99.56% of the 227 assessed ISPs maintain upstream dependencies connected to IPv6-capable transit or peering networks. This high percentage signifies that the vast majority of Indian ISPs are architecturally positioned to support IPv6, ensuring that user traffic can traverse the network using the newer protocol. The major market players, such as Reliance Jio Infocomm and Bharti Airtel, have had IPv6 deployed since 2012 and 2013 respectively, underscoring their long-term commitment.

The compliant status of nearly all major ISPs, collectively serving the vast majority of the market, has been a critical enabler for India's high national IPv6 adoption rate. These large operators, some of whom deployed IPv6 over a decade ago, possess significant experience and infrastructure maturity in handling IPv6 traffic. This widespread capability ensures that India's digital economy is largely ready at the ISP level to manage the growing demands for IPv6 connectivity.

3. Where India could improve

India's internet resilience could be significantly enhanced by addressing critical gaps in routing security, public sector digital infrastructure readiness, core domain name system (DNS) integrity, and full market ISP compliance, each of which introduces unnecessary risk and limits the full potential of IPv6 deployment.

3.1 Suboptimal RPKI Coverage for IPv6 Prefixes

Despite strong IPv6 adoption, India's IPv6 routing security is significantly underdeveloped, with only 28.66% of its 25,831 IPv6 routing prefixes covered by RPKI (Resource Public Key Infrastructure). This low coverage leaves a substantial portion of India's IPv6 address space vulnerable to route hijacking, misorigination, and other BGP (Border Gateway Protocol) routing incidents that can lead to service disruptions and security breaches. The absence of proper RPKI validation can undermine the trust and stability of internet routing within the country.

Several major networks, including VIL-AS-AP Vodafone Idea Ltd (0.00% RPKI coverage for 1135 IPv6 prefixes) and TATAPLAYBROADBAND-AS-AP TATA PLAY BROADB (0.00% RPKI coverage for 635 IPv6 prefixes), show critically weak RPKI adoption. These significant market players, among others, contribute to the overall low national RPKI coverage despite their substantial IPv6 routing presence. Rectifying these gaps is essential for bolstering the security and reliability of India's IPv6 internet infrastructure.

3.2 Low Government and Self-Hosted Domain IPv6 Readiness

The readiness of government websites for IPv6 in India is notably low, with only 13.20% of the 947 assessed `.gov.in` domains being IPv6-capable. This stark contrast with the high national user adoption rate signifies a significant gap in the public sector's digital infrastructure modernization. The reliance of 812 government domains exclusively on IPv4 not only creates potential accessibility barriers for IPv6-only users but also indicates a lag in integrating next-generation internet protocols into critical public services.

Similarly, within the broader web services ecosystem, self-hosted domains demonstrate weaker IPv6 deployment compared to CDN-backed services. While specific data for IPv4-only self-hosted domains indicates 512 out of 932 are IPv4-only, this reinforces the challenge for entities not leveraging global CDN infrastructure to adopt IPv6. The government's slow adoption in particular suggests a need for targeted policy and technical support to bring public digital services in line with the country's overall IPv6 progress.

3.3 Limited IPv6 Readiness for Core ccTLD Infrastructure

The core infrastructure supporting India's national `.in` ccTLD exhibits significant weaknesses in IPv6 readiness, particularly concerning glue records. Out of 136 glue-style nameservers assessed, only 1 is IPv6-capable, resulting in a critical Glue IPv6 Readiness score of 0.74%. This effectively indicates a near-total absence of IPv6 support for self-hosted authoritative DNS infrastructure within the `.in` domain, creating a single point of failure and increasing reliance on IPv4 for crucial domain resolution.

Furthermore, overall authoritative nameserver IPv6 reachability for the `.in` ecosystem stands at 55.04% (1135 out of 2062 nameservers). While higher than glue record readiness, this still leaves almost half of the authoritative DNS infrastructure unreachable over IPv6, impacting the resilience and performance of the national domain space. Such deficiencies in the fundamental DNS infrastructure can lead to slower resolution times for IPv6 users and potential outages if IPv4 connectivity were to be compromised or deprecated.

3.4 Unaddressed ISP Non-Compliance

While India boasts a high overall IPv6 adoption, a small but critical segment of the market remains underserved due to ISP non-compliance. Specifically, one ISP, APSFL-AS Andhra Pradesh State Fiber, covering 0.2% of the market, is classified as a "Ghost" archetype. This classification means the ISP has zero IPv6 prefixes in the global routing table, indicating a complete absence of IPv6 allocation and deployment. Such a network cannot provide IPv6 connectivity to its subscribers, creating a potential barrier to internet access for its user base as the internet transitions more fully to IPv6.

The continued existence of even a single non-compliant ISP with a measurable market share highlights a loophole in existing regulatory or industry self-governance frameworks. This situation not only impacts the resilience of the local internet but also undermines the national IPv6 transition efforts by leaving a portion of the subscriber base behind. Immediate intervention is required to ensure that all market participants acquire necessary RIR membership and deploy IPv6 prefixes.

6.1 Where policy is absent or unenforced

Gap Consequence Remedy

No regulatory mechanism to ensure universal ISP RIR membership and basic IPv6 allocation. Small pockets of the market remain completely unaddressed by IPv6, creating an uneven internet access landscape. Require all licensed ISPs to demonstrate RIR membership and minimum IPv6 allocation as a condition for operation.

6.2 Loopholes to avoid creating

  • Avoid mandating specific technical implementation details (e.g., particular vendors or software) that stifle innovation and market competition.
  • Do not create subsidies for non-compliance that reward inaction or disincentivize genuine IPv6 transition efforts.
  • Ensure that any "IPv6-only" mandates for services are phased in carefully, providing clear migration paths and avoiding immediate fragmentation for existing IPv4-only users.
  • Guard against the creation of artificial market barriers for new entrants by imposing overly burdensome IPv6 requirements without corresponding support mechanisms.
  • Avoid mandates that inadvertently create single points of failure in critical infrastructure by overly centralizing IPv6 management or resolution services.

Conclusion

India's high national IPv6 adoption rate of 78.9% is a significant achievement, positioning the country as a global leader in user-side transition to the next-generation internet protocol. This widespread deployment underscores a foundational strength in its internet ecosystem, with most major ISPs and IXPs demonstrating strong IPv6 capabilities. However, a critical disconnect persists between this impressive user adoption and the operational and security configurations of key internet infrastructure components. Areas such as RPKI coverage, government domain readiness, and ccTLD glue record support remain significantly underdeveloped, introducing unnecessary risks and limiting the full benefits of a truly resilient IPv6 internet.

The strategic pathway to enhanced resilience lies in sequencing quick, administrative wins before embarking on capital-intensive projects. Leveraging the existing high user adoption as an incentive, policy-makers can first focus on implementing low-cost, high-impact interventions such as enforcing RPKI ROA creation, mandating RIR membership for all ISPs, and driving IPv6 readiness in government services. Such configuration-centric actions build regulatory momentum and demonstrate tangible progress without requiring extensive capital outlays, paving the way for more comprehensive policy frameworks and long-term structural investments needed to secure India's internet for the future.
