1. Where India stands

India has achieved a remarkable IPv6 adoption rate of 76.4%, positioning the country at #5 globally out of 243 assessed nations. This figure is substantially higher than the global average of 24.4%, demonstrating significant progress in transitioning end-users to the next generation Internet protocol. This leadership in user adoption is a testament to the proactive deployment efforts by major Internet Service Providers within the country.

Two readings matter for policy. First, India's high user adoption indicates a robust demand-side readiness and successful large-scale deployment by key access providers. Second, the disparity between this high user adoption and the nascent IPv6 readiness of popular web services, government domains, and critical DNS infrastructure, such as glue records, highlights a crucial supply-side operational gap.

3. Where India could improve

3.4 Absence of IPv6 Glue Records

A significant deficiency identified in the .in ccTLD ecosystem is the complete absence of IPv6-capable glue records, registering at 0.00% readiness. Glue records are crucial for the resilience and performance of DNS resolution, particularly for authoritative nameservers that are "in-bailiwick" (i.e., whose hostnames fall within the same domain hierarchy as the delegated domain).

This absence implies that for domains relying on such self-hosted authoritative DNS infrastructure, resolution over IPv6 is compromised or entirely dependent on IPv4. This vulnerability introduces a single point of failure and limits the full benefit of end-to-end IPv6 connectivity, undermining the overall robustness of the national DNS infrastructure.

6.2 Loopholes to avoid creating

  • Mandating specific, rapidly evolving technical solutions rather than focusing on desired outcomes and performance metrics.
  • Imposing unfunded IPv6 deployment mandates on smaller operators or content providers without corresponding financial incentives or technical assistance programs.
  • Creating policies that inadvertently incentivize or allow content providers to rely exclusively on IPv4-to-IPv6 translation services, thereby bypassing native IPv6 content deployment.
  • Restricting innovation by overly prescriptive regulations that do not account for diverse deployment scenarios and technological advancements in IPv6 transition.
  • Overlooking the supply chain for IT equipment and software, potentially mandating IPv6 support without ensuring that vendors can meet those requirements within reasonable timeframes.

Conclusion

India has achieved significant success in user-side IPv6 adoption, demonstrating a mature and widely deployed IPv6 access infrastructure that positions the country as a global leader. This strong foundation, however, coexists with notable operational and security configuration gaps, particularly in routing security, web content readiness, and critical DNS infrastructure. The disparity between high end-user IPv6 capability and lagging content/service availability underscores a critical need to shift policy focus from simply enabling access to ensuring a fully functional, secure, and resilient end-to-end IPv6 ecosystem.

Prioritizing cheap configuration wins, such as RPKI ROA creation, IPv6 validation for government services, and educating ccTLD operators on glue records, can generate rapid, measurable improvements without extensive capital investment. These initial successes can build regulatory momentum and demonstrate the tangible benefits of a complete IPv6 transition, paving the way for more complex, capital-heavy projects in the medium to long term. By systematically addressing these identified weaknesses, India can fully leverage its impressive IPv6 user base to unlock enhanced security, resilience, and future-proof digital infrastructure across all sectors.
