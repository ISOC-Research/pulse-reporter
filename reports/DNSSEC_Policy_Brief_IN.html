Strategic Roadmap: The Policy Agenda

Phase 1 — Quick Wins
  1. Mandate DNSSEC Validation for Government & Critical Infrastructure: Issue immediate regulatory decrees requiring all government agencies, public sector undertakings, and critical infrastructure providers to enable DNSSEC validation on their recursive DNS resolvers within 6 months.
  2. Public Awareness Campaign: Launch a targeted campaign to educate enterprise IT managers, web developers, and subsequently the general public, about DNSSEC's importance and how to verify secure DNS resolution.
  3. Pilot Program for Top 3 ISPs: Initiate a pilot program with the top 3 ISPs (based on query share) to demonstrate and document successful DNSSEC validation rollout, offering technical assistance and recognition.
  4. Review and Update Regulatory Frameworks: Begin a review of existing telecommunications and internet policy frameworks to identify and remove barriers, and introduce incentives, for DNSSEC adoption.
Phase 2 — Structural
  1. Nationwide ISP DNSSEC Mandate: Introduce a phased regulatory mandate requiring all licensed ISPs to enable DNSSEC validation by default for all subscribers, with clear timelines and compliance mechanisms. This should include guidelines for handling invalid responses (e.g., "fail-safe" vs. "warn and proceed").
  2. Incentivize Enterprise Adoption: Implement tax incentives or grants for businesses and organizations that deploy DNSSEC on their domains and enable validation internally.
  3. Build Local DNSSEC Expertise: Establish national training programs and certifications for DNSSEC deployment, management, and incident response, engaging educational institutions and industry experts.
  4. Strengthen ccTLD Security: Conduct regular audits of the .in ccTLD's DNSSEC signing practices and key management, ensuring cryptographic agility and robust operational security.
Phase 3 — Vision
  1. Establish a National DNSSEC Task Force: Create a permanent, multi-stakeholder body comprising government, industry, academia, and civil society to continually monitor, promote, and evolve national DNSSEC strategy.
  2. Regional Leadership in DNS Security: Champion DNSSEC adoption initiatives in regional forums (e.g., SAARC, BIMSTEC) and offer technical assistance to neighboring countries, enhancing regional internet resilience.
  3. Research and Development: Invest in R&D for next-generation DNS security protocols and quantum-resistant cryptography, positioning the nation at the forefront of internet security innovation.
  4. Digital Sovereignty through Secure Foundations: Fully embed DNSSEC as a fundamental pillar of national digital sovereignty, ensuring that the control and integrity of the nation's online identity are protected at the most basic level.
By decisively addressing DNSSEC adoption, we can significantly enhance national cybersecurity, protect citizens from online threats, and bolster the nation's digital economy.

Final Verdict

The current state of DNSSEC validation indicates a critical gap between foundational infrastructure strength and end-user security. While the .in ccTLD is well-managed and resilient in its core, the lack of widespread DNSSEC validation by ISPs and recursive resolvers exposes the entire digital ecosystem to significant risks. Addressing this vulnerability is not merely a technical fix; it is a strategic imperative for national security, economic stability, and digital trust. Focused, phased intervention is required to elevate the nation's DNSSEC posture.
