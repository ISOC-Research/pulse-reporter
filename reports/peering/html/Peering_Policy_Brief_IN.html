The values presented in this document are the Internet Resilience Index, normalized to 1–100. These values should be interpreted as a score rather than an absolute value. In some cases, the raw absolute values are presented to provide additional context.

Executive summary

India presents a complex and dynamic Internet peering landscape, characterized by substantial foundational infrastructure juxtaposed with significant opportunities for operational enhancement and greater traffic localization. With 6,317 allocated Autonomous Systems (ASNs) and 2,973 actively routed, the nation possesses a robust base of network operators. However, the active routing ratio of 47.06% suggests a considerable portion of allocated ASNs remain unutilized or dormant, indicating potential for greater network participation. The country hosts 42 Internet Exchange Points (IXPs) and 246 peering facilities, a notable presence that underscores a foundational capacity for domestic interconnection. Despite this, a key structural paradox emerges: while the physical presence is significant, operational deployment, particularly concerning domestic peering at IXPs and traffic localization, remains suboptimal when benchmarked against best practices and the country’s scale.

Analysis of key metrics reveals a strong performance in certain areas, such as the high penetration of Tier-1 Content Delivery Networks (CDNs) at domestic IXPs (8 of 9) and within local facilities (8 of 9), which is crucial for delivering popular content closer to end-users. Route Server (RS) utilization at IXPs is also commendable at 86.65%, streamlining peering arrangements. Furthermore, India demonstrates excellent routing efficiency with a 0.0% domestic tromboning rate and a low median eyeball-to-edge latency of 13.53 ms, indicating efficient last-mile delivery and minimal unnecessary traffic redirection. However, the Domestic IXP Penetration Rate stands at 27.21%, meaning only a quarter of active domestic ASNs peer locally. This directly impacts the Traffic Localization Ratio, which is 26.60%, falling short of targets and suggesting a substantial volume of domestic traffic transits internationally. The geographic concentration of IXPs, with Mumbai hosting nearly a quarter of all national IXPs, introduces a single point of failure risk despite the HHI index of 0.1451 being just within the diversified threshold. Improving domestic peering participation and traffic localization are critical for enhancing the overall resilience and efficiency of India's Internet ecosystem.

1. Where India stands

India's Internet ecosystem demonstrates a foundational strength in its sheer number of active Autonomous Systems and installed Internet Exchange Point (IXP) infrastructure, hosting 2,973 active routed ASNs and 42 IXPs. This positions the nation as a significant player globally in terms of network presence. However, when compared against optimal benchmarks for peering and localization, India shows substantial room for improvement. The Domestic IXP Penetration Rate of 27.21% lags considerably behind the recommended >50% target for resilient domestic interconnection. Similarly, the Traffic Localization Ratio of 26.60% is well below the >50% target, indicating that a majority of domestic traffic still transits through international pathways rather than being exchanged locally, which can introduce unnecessary latency and reduce resilience.

While the country benefits from a robust presence of Tier-1 Content Delivery Networks (8 of 9) within its domestic IXPs and facilities, and exhibits an impressively low domestic tromboning rate of 0.0%, these efficiencies are somewhat undermined by the overall low domestic peering uptake. The geographic distribution of IXPs, while not severely concentrated with an HHI of 0.1451 (just within the <0.15 diversified threshold), still shows a leaning towards major metropolitan areas like Mumbai, which hosts nearly a quarter of all IXPs. This centralisation, combined with low domestic peering, means that even localized content may not always be optimally routed within the country, potentially increasing costs and reducing the overall resilience of the national Internet infrastructure.

Two readings matter for policy. First, the Domestic IXP Penetration Rate, currently at 27.21%, highlights a critical underutilization of the existing 42 IXPs. This suggests a significant number of domestic ASNs, despite being active, are not leveraging local peering opportunities, possibly due to lack of awareness, technical expertise, or business incentives. Second, the Traffic Localization Ratio, at 26.60%, reinforces this gap, indicating that a substantial proportion of traffic exchanged between domestic users and content providers still leaves the country before returning, adding to latency and reducing the economic benefits of local exchange. Addressing these two areas offers the most significant leverage for enhancing India's Internet resilience.

2. Where India is improving

India demonstrates strong performance in several key areas crucial for Internet resilience, particularly concerning content delivery and routing efficiency. The nation has been successful in attracting major global content providers, securing a high presence of Tier-1 CDNs within its borders. This ensures that much of the most popular Internet content is cached locally, significantly reducing latency for end-users and improving overall network experience.

2.1 Robust Tier-1 CDN Presence

India has successfully attracted 8 of the 9 global Tier-1 Content Delivery Networks (CDNs) to establish a presence within its domestic Internet Exchange Points (IXPs) and local data center facilities. This substantial footprint is critical for localizing high-demand content, reducing traffic tromboning, and lowering latency for users. The presence of major content providers peering locally enhances the overall quality and resilience of the national Internet infrastructure by keeping traffic within the country.

2.2 High Route Server Utilization and Peering Facilities

India's IXPs exhibit a high rate of Route Server (RS) utilization, with 86.65% of ASNs that peer at local IXPs also utilizing RS services. This high adoption significantly simplifies peering arrangements, allowing networks to peer with multiple participants via a single BGP session, which is crucial for fostering an efficient and scalable peering ecosystem. Furthermore, the country boasts 246 peering facilities, providing ample physical locations for networks and content providers to interconnect.

The effective use of Route Servers by 701 ASNs, representing 86.65% of ASNs peering at local IXPs, showcases operational maturity and a willingness among operators to adopt practices that reduce the administrative burden of peering. This, combined with the extensive footprint of 246 peering facilities, indicates that the physical and logical infrastructure to facilitate broad-scale domestic interconnection is largely in place. Capitalizing on this strong foundation by encouraging more ASNs to join IXPs and utilize RS capabilities represents a natural next step.

2.3 Excellent Routing Efficiency and Low Latency

India demonstrates exemplary routing efficiency, achieving a 0.00% domestic tromboning (boomerang routing) rate across 15 analyzed traceroute paths. This indicates that traffic between domestic points is consistently routed optimally within the country without unnecessary diversions through international transit. Concurrently, the median eyeball-to-edge latency is an impressive 13.53 ms, indicating rapid access to content and services for the average user. This performance is well within global best-practice targets, showcasing robust network design and effective local caching.

The complete absence of domestic tromboning is a significant achievement, reflecting well-designed network paths and peering strategies that prevent inefficient routing loops. This, alongside the low median latency, highlights that when traffic is exchanged domestically, it traverses an efficient and well-performing network. These metrics confirm that the underlying network infrastructure can deliver high-quality, low-latency services, provided that traffic is directed to leverage these domestic paths effectively.

3. Where India could improve

India's Internet resilience could be significantly bolstered by addressing key weaknesses related to domestic peering participation, traffic localization, IXP transparency, and infrastructure distribution, each contributing to a degree of fragility and inefficiency.

3.1 Suboptimal Domestic IXP Penetration

Despite a significant number of active Autonomous Systems (ASNs) and 42 operational Internet Exchange Points (IXPs), India's Domestic IXP Penetration Rate stands at only 27.21%. This means that over two-thirds of active domestic ASNs (2,164 out of 2,973) are not actively peering at local IXPs. Such low participation limits the benefits of local interconnection, leading to increased reliance on international transit, higher operational costs for networks, and reduced overall network resilience due to fewer redundant local paths.

The underutilization of existing IXP infrastructure represents a significant missed opportunity. While 809 domestic ASNs do peer locally, the majority do not, which inevitably pushes traffic to higher-cost, often international, transit providers even when domestic peers are available. Increasing this penetration rate is fundamental to fostering a more robust and economically efficient domestic Internet ecosystem, by bringing more domestic traffic onto local exchange points.

3.2 Insufficient Traffic Localization

The Traffic Localization Ratio for India is 26.60%, falling significantly short of the >50% target for optimal domestic traffic exchange. This metric indicates that a substantial portion of Internet traffic originating and terminating within India still traverses international routes or is not exchanged efficiently within the country. Low localization often results in higher latency for domestic content, increased operational costs for service providers, and reduced resilience to international cable cuts or geopolitical disruptions.

The low localization ratio, despite a strong Tier-1 CDN presence, suggests that while popular global content is localized, much of the domestic-to-domestic traffic or non-Tier-1 content still takes suboptimal paths. This implies that while the capacity for localization exists, the operational and policy frameworks to consistently achieve it for all types of traffic are not yet fully effective. Enhancing domestic peering, particularly for local content hosts and smaller networks, is crucial to improve this ratio.

3.3 Limited IXP Traffic Telemetry Reporting

Out of 32 domestic IXPs listed by Packet Clearing House (PCH), only 18 (56.25%) are actively reporting traffic telemetry data. A significant number of IXPs, including major ones such as DE-CIX Mumbai, NIXI Mumbai, and the majority of NIXI exchanges, report zero or no traffic data to public aggregators. This lack of transparency impedes accurate analysis of traffic flows, hinders informed decision-making for network planning and peering negotiations, and makes it challenging to identify underperforming or underutilized exchange points.

3.4 Centralised IXP Infrastructure

Despite having 42 IXPs distributed across 12 cities, India's IXP infrastructure exhibits a degree of geographic concentration. Mumbai hosts 10 IXPs, representing 23.81% of the national total. Delhi follows with 8 IXPs (19.05%), and Kolkata with 6 (14.29%). This concentration in a few major metropolitan areas, while contributing to a functional HHI of 0.1451, implies that a significant portion of domestic interconnection relies on these key hubs. Over-reliance on a few locations can introduce single points of failure, making the national Internet more vulnerable to localized disruptions such as power outages or natural disasters affecting these major cities.

6. Policy gaps and loopholes

6.1 Where policy is absent or unenforced

Gap Consequence Remedy
Lack of a clear, overarching national peering policy. Inconsistent peering practices, reduced domestic traffic exchange, and greater reliance on costly international transit for domestic traffic. Develop and promulgate a national peering strategy that outlines principles for open and equitable interconnection.
Absence of mandatory public traffic telemetry reporting for IXPs. Opaque peering market, difficulty in assessing IXP performance, and hindered data-driven policy making and network planning. Mandate or incentivize all operational IXPs to publish anonymized, aggregated traffic data in real-time or near real-time.
Insufficient incentives for domestic content hosting and caching. Content providers prioritize international hosting, leading to suboptimal traffic localization despite local CDN presence. Introduce financial or regulatory incentives for domestic content providers to host and cache within India.
Limited regulatory clarity on IXP ownership and operational models. Uncertainty for potential IXP investors and operators, potentially stifling the growth of new, independent IXPs. Publish clear guidelines and licensing frameworks for various IXP operational models (e.g., non-profit, commercial, hybrid).

6.2 Loopholes to avoid creating

  • Mandating peering: Imposing mandatory peering obligations can distort market dynamics, burden smaller networks, and disincentivize innovation, potentially leading to lower quality of service.
  • Centralizing IXP management: Consolidating all IXPs under a single government entity or monopolistic provider can stifle competition, innovation, and local responsiveness, creating a single point of failure.
  • Disincentivizing private peering: Policies that exclusively promote IXP peering without acknowledging the role of private peering can lead to inefficiencies for large operators and bypass beneficial direct connections.
  • Imposing excessive regulatory burdens on IXPs: Overly complex licensing requirements or technical mandates can deter new IXP entrants and increase operational costs for existing ones, hindering growth.
  • Favoring specific IXP models or operators: Preferential treatment for certain types of IXPs (e.g., non-profit vs. commercial) or existing players can create unfair competition and limit market diversity.

Conclusion

India's Internet infrastructure demonstrates a paradox of substantial physical network presence and a yet-to-be-fully-realized operational potential for domestic peering. While the country benefits from an impressive count of Internet Exchange Points, high Tier-1 CDN penetration, and excellent routing efficiency with minimal domestic tromboning, the low domestic IXP penetration rate and traffic localization ratio indicate a significant disconnect between capability and deployment. A substantial portion of active domestic ASNs are not leveraging local peering opportunities, leading to unnecessary international transit for domestic traffic and suboptimal resilience.

The proposed staged action plan prioritizes quick-win interventions that require minimal capital expenditure, focusing instead on configuration changes, administrative coordination, and targeted outreach. By first addressing these low-effort, high-impact areas such as boosting IXP onboarding, improving telemetry reporting, and optimizing peering policies, India can rapidly improve its core peering metrics. This sequential approach builds crucial regulatory momentum and demonstrates tangible progress, paving the way for more significant, capital-heavy structural investments in new IXPs, resilient fiber backhaul, and comprehensive national peering policies in the medium to long term. Such a strategic sequencing ensures that foundational efficiencies are established before expanding physical capacity, leading to a more robust, cost-effective, and resilient Internet for all users in India.
