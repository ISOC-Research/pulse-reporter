Executive summary

India presents a complex and evolving peering landscape, characterised by a significant foundational infrastructure alongside opportunities for enhanced domestic interconnection. The nation accounts for 6317 allocated autonomous systems (ASNs), with 2972 (47.05%) actively routed in BGP, indicating a large pool of network operators. Physical infrastructure is extensive, comprising 42 Internet Exchange Points (IXPs) and 246 peering facilities, with 40 (95.24%) of the listed IXPs actively supporting member networks. This robust physical presence positions India favourably for widespread domestic traffic exchange. However, the domestic ASN IXP penetration rate stands at 27.19%, suggesting that a substantial majority of active ASNs are yet to leverage local IXPs for peering, potentially leading to inefficient traffic paths and higher costs.

Operational metrics reveal both strengths and areas for improvement. A high route server utilization rate of 86.63% among connected ASNs at IXPs indicates efficient and streamlined peering practices for those actively participating. The presence of 8 out of 9 Tier-1 Content Delivery Networks (CDNs) at domestic IXPs and within local facilities further contributes to content localization and reduced latency for end-users. Despite these assets, the overall traffic localization ratio is 26.60%, implying that a significant portion of traffic either transits internationally or through private arrangements that may not be optimally localised. This low localization, combined with a median eyeball edge latency of 12.84 ms and a commendable 0.0% domestic tromboning rate, underscores the potential for substantial gains in efficiency and resilience through increased domestic peering and enhanced policy frameworks. Addressing the gap in IXP penetration and improving the overall traffic localization ratio are critical for India to fully realise the benefits of its extensive peering infrastructure.

1. Where India stands

India's peering ecosystem is characterised by a significant and expanding infrastructure base, yet it faces the challenge of under-optimised utilisation among its vast number of network operators. With 6317 allocated Autonomous Systems (ASNs), of which 2972 (47.05%) are actively routed in BGP, the country possesses a substantial foundation of potential peering participants. This is complemented by 42 Internet Exchange Points (IXPs) across 12 cities and 246 peering facilities, demonstrating a strong commitment to physical interconnection. Forty of these IXPs (95.24%) are actively connecting networks, indicating a high operational readiness within the established infrastructure. However, the domestic ASN IXP penetration rate stands at 27.19%, meaning that over 70% of active domestic ASNs are not directly participating in local IXPs, which represents a considerable untapped potential for improving local traffic exchange efficiency.

The distribution of IXPs shows a degree of concentration, with Mumbai hosting 10 IXPs (23.81% of the national total), followed by Delhi, Kolkata, and Chennai. While this provides strong interconnection hubs, it also highlights an opportunity for fostering growth in other regions to enhance overall network resilience and reduce regional disparities in access to efficient peering. Despite the moderate IXP penetration, 8 out of 9 Tier-1 Content Delivery Networks (CDNs) are actively peering at domestic IXPs and are present in local facilities, ensuring that a significant portion of high-demand content is localised. The median eyeball edge latency of 12.84 ms and a 0.0% domestic tromboning rate further indicate that when traffic is localised, it is routed efficiently within the country. This efficient local routing capability, however, is not fully leveraged, as evidenced by a traffic localization ratio of 26.60%, suggesting a large volume of domestic traffic either transits abroad or bypasses local IXPs.

Two readings matter for policy. First, the low domestic ASN IXP penetration rate of 27.19% signals that a large segment of India's active network operators are not yet benefiting from the cost and performance advantages of local interconnection. This indicates a need for targeted outreach, education, and potentially policy incentives to encourage greater participation. Second, the modest traffic localization ratio of 26.60%, despite extensive infrastructure and strong CDN presence, highlights a systemic challenge in keeping domestic traffic within national borders. This suggests that while physical capacity exists, the operational practices and policy environment may not yet fully support optimal in-country traffic exchange, leading to potential leakages or reliance on more expensive transit options.

2. Where India is improving

2.1 Robust Internet Exchange Point Infrastructure

India has established a substantial foundation for peering and interconnection, with a total of 42 Internet Exchange Points (IXPs) across 12 cities. This extensive deployment of IXP infrastructure underscores a strong commitment to facilitating domestic traffic exchange. Critically, 40 of these IXPs (95.24%) are operational and actively connecting networks, indicating a high level of readiness and an environment conducive to further growth in local peering. The presence of 246 peering facilities further augments this infrastructure, providing numerous physical points of presence for network operators and content providers to interconnect.

The geographic distribution, while showing some concentration, still ensures that major metropolitan areas are well-served. Mumbai, Delhi, Kolkata, Chennai, and Hyderabad collectively host a significant proportion of the IXPs, creating key hubs for interconnection. This infrastructure depth is a critical asset for building a more resilient and efficient national internet ecosystem, providing the physical backbone for content localisation and reduced latency across the country. The availability of multiple IXPs in major cities also fosters competition and provides choices for network operators.

2.2 High Route Server Utilization

Among the domestic ASNs that do peer at local IXPs, there is a notably high adoption of route servers, with 700 ASNs utilising them. This translates to a route server utilization rate of 86.63%. This metric indicates that a vast majority of participating networks are leveraging best practices for efficient peering. Route servers simplify the peering process by allowing a single BGP session to exchange routes with multiple participants, thereby reducing administrative overhead and operational complexity for IXP members.

The high utilization rate demonstrates an understanding and acceptance of modern peering methodologies within India's IXP community. This operational efficiency is a significant strength, as it encourages smaller and newer ASNs to join IXPs by lowering the technical barrier to entry. It also fosters a more robust and interconnected fabric by enabling more networks to peer with each other indirectly, even if they do not establish direct bilateral sessions with every other participant.

3. Where India could improve

India's peering ecosystem faces several key bottlenecks that limit its full potential for resilience, primarily stemming from low domestic IXP participation, suboptimal traffic localization, and geographic concentration of interconnection infrastructure.

3.1 Suboptimal Domestic IXP Penetration

Despite the large number of active Autonomous Systems (ASNs) in India (2972), the domestic IXP penetration rate stands at a modest 27.19%. This indicates that a significant majority of active domestic ASNs (approximately 2164 ASNs) are not directly peering at local Internet Exchange Points. This under-utilisation of existing IXP infrastructure can lead to several inefficiencies, including reliance on more expensive upstream transit providers, increased latency for domestic traffic that may traverse international routes, and reduced overall network resilience due to fewer local interconnection points.

The low penetration rate suggests a gap in awareness, perceived value, or accessibility for many smaller and medium-sized network operators. While the high route server utilization among current IXP members demonstrates efficient peering practices for those engaged, the challenge remains in attracting a larger proportion of the active ASN community to participate. Increased participation would not only enhance local traffic exchange but also foster a more competitive and robust internet ecosystem within India.

3.2 Low Traffic Localization Ratio

The traffic localization ratio for India is currently 26.60%, alongside a peering efficiency score of 0.2660. This metric is critical as it reflects the proportion of domestic traffic that is exchanged within the country's borders rather than transiting internationally. A low localization ratio suggests that a significant amount of traffic destined for users within India, or originating from them, may be routing abroad even when a local path is available. While the presence of Tier-1 CDNs helps localise content, the overall low ratio indicates that inter-operator peering arrangements may not be fully optimized at the national level.

Suboptimal traffic localization can lead to higher operational costs for ISPs due to increased transit expenses, degraded user experience through higher latency, and reduced national internet resilience. It implies that despite the physical infrastructure being present, the economic or policy incentives for domestic peering may not be sufficiently strong, or there might be technical impediments preventing a higher proportion of traffic from being exchanged locally. Improving this ratio is fundamental to strengthening India's internet resilience and economic efficiency.

3.3 Geographic Concentration of IXPs

While India boasts 42 IXPs across 12 cities, there is a notable geographic concentration of these critical interconnection points. Mumbai, for instance, hosts 10 IXPs, representing 23.81% of the national total. Delhi, Kolkata, and Chennai also serve as significant hubs. This concentration, while creating strong central points for peering, can introduce single points of failure risk and may limit the accessibility and benefits of local peering for network operators and users in less-served regions. The Herfindahl-Hirschman Index (HHI) for geographic concentration is 0.1451, which indicates a moderate level of concentration.

A more distributed IXP presence across a wider array of cities, particularly in emerging regional centers, could significantly enhance the resilience of the national internet infrastructure. It would reduce reliance on a few major cities for critical interconnection, improve local latency in underserved areas, and potentially drive up domestic IXP penetration by bringing peering points closer to more network operators. Encouraging the establishment and growth of IXPs in Tier-2 and Tier-3 cities is essential for ensuring equitable access to efficient internet services and strengthening overall national internet resilience.

6. Policy gaps and loopholes

6.1 Where policy is absent or unenforced

Gap Consequence Remedy
Absence of a comprehensive national peering strategy or policy. Suboptimal domestic traffic localization, inefficient routing, and higher transit costs for ISPs. Develop and implement a clear national peering policy that encourages local interconnection and outlines principles for fair access.

Introduce financial incentives (e.g., tax breaks, grants) or regulatory mandates for specific classes of ISPs to connect to local IXPs.
Inconsistent or complex regulatory frameworks for new IXP establishment and operation. Slows the growth of new IXPs, particularly in underserved regions, perpetuating geographic concentration. Streamline the licensing and operational requirements for IXPs, ensuring neutrality and ease of establishment.

6.2 Loopholes to avoid creating

  • Mandating peering at IXPs without ensuring IXP neutrality, open governance, and fair access for all participants, which could lead to anti-competitive practices or favouritism.
  • Imposing data localization requirements without simultaneously encouraging and facilitating the localisation of content caches and robust domestic peering infrastructure, potentially leading to inefficient and costly data tromboning within national borders.
  • Implementing policies that disproportionately burden smaller ISPs or new IXP entrants with excessive fees or complex compliance requirements, thereby hindering competition and growth in the peering ecosystem.
  • Creating a regulatory environment that incentivises private peering over open IXP peering without clear oversight, which could reduce transparency and diminish the benefits of a collective interconnection platform.

Conclusion

India's peering ecosystem presents a compelling narrative of significant physical infrastructure contrasted with an under-optimised operational deployment. The country boasts an impressive number of Internet Exchange Points and peering facilities, coupled with a large pool of active Autonomous Systems. This foundational strength provides an excellent platform for fostering a resilient and efficient national internet. However, the current low domestic IXP penetration rate and the modest traffic localization ratio indicate a crucial disconnect between the availability of infrastructure and its full utilization by network operators. Bridging this gap is paramount for unlocking the economic and resilience benefits of local interconnection.

The proposed staged plan emphasises that achieving substantial improvements in peering resilience does not solely rely on capital-intensive projects. Instead, it highlights the importance of sequencing cheap, configuration-based and administrative "quick wins" – such as promoting IXP participation and streamlining onboarding – before embarking on long-term structural investments. These initial, low-effort interventions can rapidly build regulatory momentum, increase operator engagement, and provide tangible evidence of progress. By focusing on policy reform, educational outreach, and incentivisation, India can create a virtuous cycle that encourages greater domestic peering, improves traffic localization, and ultimately enhances the overall resilience and efficiency of its internet infrastructure for all users.
