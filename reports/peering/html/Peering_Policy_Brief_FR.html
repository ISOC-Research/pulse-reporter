How to interpret the numbers in this report?
The values presented in this document are the Internet Resilience Index, normalized to 1–100. These values should be interpreted as a score rather than an absolute value. In some cases, the raw absolute values are presented to provide additional context.

Executive summary

France presents a well-developed internet infrastructure landscape, characterized by a substantial number of active autonomous systems (ASNs) and a comprehensive physical network. With 1,497 actively routed ASNs out of 1,898 allocated, the nation demonstrates a significant operational base. The presence of 27 Internet Exchange Points (IXPs) and 227 peering facilities across 17 cities establishes a robust foundation for interconnection. Furthermore, the excellent presence of Tier-1 Content Delivery Networks (CDNs) – with all 9 present in domestic facilities and 8 of 9 peering at domestic IXPs – ensures efficient delivery of international content to end-users. Despite this extensive infrastructure, a notable structural paradox exists: the country's inherent capability, evidenced by physical infrastructure and global CDN presence, is not fully translated into optimized domestic peering efficiency. This is highlighted by a relatively low Domestic IXP Penetration Rate and Traffic Localization Ratio, suggesting untapped potential for in-country traffic exchange.

Key metrics underscore both strengths and areas for improvement. The 25.58% Domestic IXP Penetration Rate, with only 383 of 1,497 active ASNs peering at local IXPs, indicates that a significant portion of domestic traffic may still traverse international or longer paths, potentially increasing latency and transit costs. This is compounded by a Traffic Localization Ratio of 23.33%, which suggests a substantial volume of traffic exits the country unnecessarily before returning. While Route Server Utilization at IXPs stands at a healthy 77.28% for connected networks, enhancing overall IXP membership remains critical. Positively, the 0.0% Domestic Tromboning Rate, coupled with a median eyeball edge latency of 10.77 ms, demonstrates highly efficient routing for existing localized traffic and proximity to cached content. The geographic concentration index (HHI) of 0.1413, while diversified, points to Paris's continued dominance as a primary IXP hub (33.33% of IXPs), which, if unchecked, could lead to a single point of failure. These dynamics imply that while the physical infrastructure for peering is largely in place and international content delivery is highly optimized, operational strategies to encourage broader domestic interconnection and traffic localization are not yet fully leveraged.

1. Where France stands

France demonstrates a strong foundation in internet infrastructure, possessing a significant number of active Autonomous Systems (ASNs), a robust count of Internet Exchange Points (IXPs), and an extensive network of peering facilities. The country's infrastructure readiness is further bolstered by the near-complete presence of Tier-1 Content Delivery Networks (CDNs) within its borders, ensuring efficient delivery of global content. This advanced physical and content-delivery infrastructure positions France favorably in the regional and global peering landscape, reflecting a high capability for internet resilience.

However, despite these foundational strengths, France's performance in operational peering metrics, particularly Domestic IXP Penetration and Traffic Localization, indicates a notable gap. The current Domestic IXP Penetration Rate of 25.58% falls considerably short of the ISOC-recommended target of over 50%, suggesting that a substantial proportion of domestic internet traffic may not be exchanging locally. Similarly, a Traffic Localization Ratio of 23.33% indicates that much of the traffic originating and terminating within France still traverses international routes. This underutilization of local peering capacity, despite ample infrastructure, presents an opportunity for strategic interventions to enhance the efficiency and resilience of the national internet ecosystem.

Two readings matter for policy. First, France exhibits exceptional performance in key metrics related to content availability and routing efficiency. The presence of 8 out of 9 Tier-1 CDNs peering at domestic IXPs and all 9 in domestic facilities highlights a strong commitment to local content caching and delivery, directly contributing to the very low median eyeball edge latency of 10.77 ms and the ideal 0.00% domestic tromboning rate. These indicators demonstrate that when traffic is exchanged or cached locally, it is done so with high efficiency and minimal delay. Second, the relatively low Domestic IXP Penetration Rate (25.58%) and Traffic Localization Ratio (23.33%) suggest that many domestic ASNs are not leveraging the existing local IXP infrastructure for direct peering, resulting in traffic potentially incurring unnecessary transit costs or longer paths. This indicates a need for policy and outreach initiatives to encourage greater participation in the domestic peering ecosystem, thereby maximizing the benefits of the country's extensive physical infrastructure.

2. Where France is improving

2.1 Robust Content Delivery Network Presence

France benefits from a comprehensive presence of major global Content Delivery Networks (CDNs). All nine Tier-1 CDN providers maintain a physical presence within domestic data center facilities, ensuring that end-users have proximate access to a vast array of popular internet content. This extensive deployment minimizes latency for internationally sourced content, contributing significantly to a superior user experience.

Beyond physical presence, eight of the nine Tier-1 CDNs actively peer at domestic Internet Exchange Points (IXPs). This direct interconnection at local exchange points bypasses longer transit routes, further optimizing content delivery within France. The strategic decision by these global content providers to engage in local peering underscores the commercial viability and operational efficiency of France's IXP ecosystem, making it a critical hub for content distribution.

2.2 Low Domestic Tromboning and Efficient Edge Latency

France demonstrates exceptional routing efficiency for domestic traffic, evidenced by a 0.0% Domestic Tromboning Rate. This indicates that local internet paths are configured optimally, preventing traffic from unnecessarily leaving and re-entering the country's borders. Such efficient routing is critical for minimizing latency and ensuring data sovereignty, reflecting a high degree of technical competence within the network operator community.

The country also achieves an excellent Median Eyeball Edge Latency of 10.77 ms. This low latency figure suggests that end-users are very close to content and service delivery points, whether through local caching, efficient peering, or direct interconnection. The combination of zero tromboning and low latency underscores a well-engineered network architecture that prioritizes quick and direct access to internet services for the population.

3. Where France could improve

France could significantly enhance its internet resilience by addressing key bottlenecks in domestic peering participation, traffic localization, and geographic diversification, each costing a measure of operational efficiency and potential for improved user experience.

3.1 Suboptimal Domestic IXP Penetration

The Domestic IXP Penetration Rate in France stands at 25.58%, meaning only 383 of 1,497 active domestic ASNs are peering at local Internet Exchange Points. This figure is considerably lower than best practice targets and indicates that a large majority of domestic networks are not directly exchanging traffic locally. The consequence is that traffic originating and terminating within France often takes longer, more costly paths via upstream transit providers, rather than utilizing direct, efficient local interconnections.

Such low penetration limits the benefits of local peering, including reduced latency, lower operational costs for network operators, and enhanced resilience against international route failures. Encouraging more domestic ASNs to connect and peer at local IXPs is a fundamental step towards creating a more robust, efficient, and cost-effective internet ecosystem within the country.

3.2 Low Traffic Localization Ratio

France exhibits a Traffic Localization Ratio of 23.33%. This metric suggests that a substantial proportion of internet traffic that could technically be exchanged within the country's borders is instead routed internationally before returning to a domestic destination. This "tromboning" or "boomerang routing" for domestic traffic, while not due to network misconfiguration as shown by the 0% domestic tromboning rate, indicates a broader systemic issue related to peering policies, commercial incentives, or content hosting preferences.

A low localization ratio leads to increased reliance on international transit, higher operational costs for network providers, and potentially higher latency for domestic users, even if the median edge latency remains good for cached content. Improving this ratio requires a multi-faceted approach, including fostering local content hosting, encouraging greater domestic peering, and potentially revisiting economic incentives for local traffic exchange.

3.3 Potential for Geographic Concentration

While France has IXPs in 17 cities, its primary IXP hub remains Paris, hosting 9 (33.33%) of the national IXPs. The Geographic Concentration Index (HHI) for IXPs is 0.1413, which, while diversified below the 0.15 threshold for high concentration, indicates that Paris still holds a significant proportion of the nation's interconnection infrastructure. This concentration, though not yet critical, warrants careful monitoring.

A continued reliance on a single metropolitan area for a disproportionate share of interconnection poses potential risks, including increased vulnerability to localized outages or disasters. Promoting the growth and strengthening of IXPs in secondary cities across France would enhance the geographic resilience of the internet infrastructure, ensuring that a wider distribution of traffic exchange points supports a more robust and decentralized network.

3.4 Limited IXP Telemetry Reporting

Out of the 19 domestic IXPs listed by Packet Clearing House (PCH), only 14 actively report traffic telemetry data. This means that a significant portion of domestic IXP traffic volume remains unreported, hindering comprehensive analysis of the national peering ecosystem's health and growth. Transparent traffic data is crucial for IXP operators to attract new members, for policymakers to understand market dynamics, and for researchers to track internet development.

The lack of universal telemetry reporting obscures the true scale and efficiency of traffic exchange within France. It impedes the ability to accurately assess the impact of policy interventions aimed at improving peering and localization. Encouraging all active IXPs to consistently report traffic statistics would provide invaluable insights and foster a more data-driven approach to internet development.

6.2 Loopholes to avoid creating

  • Avoid mandating specific peering arrangements: Regulatory intervention should foster an environment for voluntary, commercial peering rather than imposing top-down mandates that can distort market dynamics and discourage innovation.
  • Do not dictate IXP locations: While promoting regional development, avoid prescriptive mandates on where IXPs must be established, as this can lead to uneconomic deployments and stifle market-driven growth.
  • Refrain from arbitrary traffic localization quotas: Setting specific percentages for in-country traffic localization can lead to artificial traffic redirection, potentially increasing latency and operational complexity, rather than genuinely improving efficiency.
  • Avoid subsidizing specific operators or IXPs: Direct subsidies to individual entities can create unfair competition, distort market incentives, and hinder the organic growth of a competitive peering ecosystem.
  • Do not impose overly complex or burdensome reporting requirements: While data transparency is key, regulatory mandates for reporting should be proportionate and practical, avoiding excessive administrative burdens that could deter IXP operation or growth.

Conclusion

France's internet infrastructure is physically robust and well-equipped with numerous IXPs and peering facilities, complemented by an excellent presence of global Content Delivery Networks. This robust physical network, however, currently exhibits a disconnect with its operational and security configurations, particularly concerning domestic peering and traffic localization. The low Domestic IXP Penetration Rate and Traffic Localization Ratio suggest that a significant portion of in-country traffic is not leveraging the existing local interconnection points, indicating an underutilization of inherent capabilities and a potential reliance on more costly or less direct international routes.

Addressing these challenges requires a strategic sequencing of interventions. By prioritizing "quick wins" – low-effort, high-impact configuration and administrative actions – France can achieve measurable improvements in domestic peering penetration and data transparency without significant capital expenditure. These initial successes can build crucial regulatory and industry momentum, paving the way for more capital-intensive, long-term structural investments that aim to decentralize interconnection further, enhance overall traffic localization, and solidify France's position as a truly resilient and efficient internet hub.
