Executive Summary: The "Big Picture" Diagnosis

Sir/Ma'am,

This report synthesizes the state of IPv6 deployment and associated security posture for our nation. We stand at a critical juncture: our country has achieved a remarkable national IPv6 adoption rate of 78.4%, ranking us #6 globally. This positions us as a leader in user-side transition to the next generation of internet protocol. However, this success masks significant vulnerabilities and compliance gaps within our core infrastructure and digital content ecosystem.

The paradox is clear: while our citizens and many ISPs have embraced IPv6, the foundational security mechanisms and a substantial portion of our national digital content, particularly government services, remain alarmingly reliant on legacy IPv4 or suffer from inadequate IPv6 security. This creates a disconnect where a highly adopted protocol operates on an insecure or incomplete backend, posing risks to national resilience and digital sovereignty.

Specifically, a mere 28.66% of IPv6 routing prefixes are secured by RPKI, leaving critical internet routes vulnerable to hijacking. Furthermore, our government digital infrastructure exhibits a concerning IPv6 readiness of only 13.20%, and crucial authoritative DNS glue records for our national domain (.in) show a dismal 0.74% IPv6 capability. These are not merely technical oversights; they represent fundamental security flaws that can be exploited for geopolitical ends, impacting national security, economic stability, and public trust. Urgent, coordinated policy intervention is required to bridge this gap and secure our digital future.

SWOT Analysis: The Strategic Cheat Sheet

💪 Strengths

  • High National IPv6 Adoption: 78.4% places us among global leaders, demonstrating strong user-side readiness and ISP commitment to the transition.
  • Global Recognition: Our #6 global ranking provides a strong platform for regional and international digital leadership.
  • Extensive IXP IPv6 LANs: 85% of domestic IXPs are IPv6-ready, indicating a robust foundation for inter-network peering.
  • Early Adopter ISPs: Major ISPs have significant IPv6 deployment experience, some dating back over a decade.

⚠️ Weaknesses

  • Alarmingly Low RPKI Coverage: Only 28.66% of IPv6 prefixes are RPKI-secured, leaving our routing infrastructure highly vulnerable to hijacking and foreign interference.
  • Poor Government Website Readiness: Only 13.20% of government domains are IPv6-capable, severely hindering public service delivery and digital sovereignty in a dual-stack future.
  • Critical DNS Vulnerability: A mere 0.74% IPv6 readiness for ccTLD authoritative DNS glue records represents a profound single point of failure and resilience risk.
  • Non-Compliant ISPs: A small but critical segment (0.2% market share) of ISPs remain without IPv6 prefix allocation, requiring immediate regulatory action.
  • Lagging Content Ecosystem: Overall web IPv6 readiness (26.00%) is significantly lower than user adoption, creating a mismatch in a fully IPv6 environment.

🚀 Opportunities

  • Leverage High User Adoption: Drive demand for IPv6-native content and services, accelerating ecosystem-wide transition.
  • Position for Digital Sovereignty: Secure our internet infrastructure against external threats, enhancing national control over digital assets.
  • Regional Leadership: Share best practices and offer expertise to peer nations, solidifying our geopolitical standing in the digital sphere.
  • Foster Innovation: Encourage the development of new services and applications designed for an IPv6-native internet.
  • AI & Nearshoring Integration: Future-proof digital infrastructure to support advanced technologies and attract high-value digital investments.

🛡️ Threats

  • Cyber Warfare & Espionage: Weak RPKI and DNS security make us susceptible to state-sponsored attacks, data exfiltration, and critical infrastructure disruption.
  • Economic Disruption: Routing insecurities and reliance on legacy protocols can lead to service outages, financial instability, and loss of investor confidence.
  • Digital Colonialism: Continued reliance on foreign-hosted or IPv4-only content and services risks ceding control of our digital economy and data.
  • Erosion of Public Trust: Government's inability to provide secure, modern digital services over IPv6 can undermine citizen confidence in public institutions.
  • Technical Debt Accumulation: Delaying comprehensive IPv6 adoption across all layers increases future costs and complexity, hindering technological agility.

Strategic Roadmap: The Policy Agenda

Phase 1 — Quick Wins
  1. Mandate RPKI Adoption: Issue a regulatory decree requiring all major ISPs (e.g., those with >0.5% market share) to achieve at least 80% RPKI coverage for their IPv6 prefixes within 6 months. Implement strict penalties for non-compliance.
  2. Address "Ghost" ISPs: Immediately engage the ISP identified as "Ghost (No Allocation)" (e.g., AS133287 - APSFL-AS) to ensure RIR membership and IPv6 prefix acquisition, bringing 0.2% of the market online.
  3. Critical Infrastructure Security Audit: Conduct an immediate, focused audit of the national ccTLD (.in) DNS authoritative nameservers, prioritizing the remediation of IPv6 glue record readiness (currently 0.74%).
  4. Secure Government DNS: Implement an executive order mandating that all new and renewed government domain registrations (.gov.in) must be dual-stack with correctly configured IPv6 glue records and AAAA records.
Phase 2 — Structural
  1. Incentivize Government IPv6 Transition: Launch a nationwide program to accelerate IPv6 adoption for all government digital services and websites, targeting 75% readiness within 24 months. Provide technical assistance and funding.
  2. Regulatory Framework Update: Review and update existing telecommunications and internet policy frameworks to embed IPv6-only readiness as a baseline for new services and infrastructure, moving beyond dual-stack as the ultimate goal.
  3. Promote CDN & Cloud Adoption: Encourage domestic content providers, particularly those currently self-hosting, to leverage IPv6-capable CDN and cloud platforms to rapidly improve web IPv6 readiness.
  4. Cybersecurity Capacity Building: Invest in national cybersecurity training programs focusing on IPv6-specific threats, RPKI management, and DNS security for network operators, government IT staff, and critical infrastructure providers.
  5. Market Diversification for IXPs: Encourage greater competition and peering at IXPs, particularly for networks with lower IPv6 peering percentages, to enhance routing resilience.
Phase 3 — Vision
  1. National IPv6 Education & Skill Development: Establish long-term educational initiatives at universities and vocational training centers to cultivate a skilled workforce proficient in IPv6 network design, security, and application development.
  2. Global Digital Leadership: Champion IPv6 adoption and internet resilience standards on international forums, showcasing our nation's progress and offering expertise to developing countries.
  3. IPv6-Native Content Ecosystem: Implement policies to incentivize the creation and hosting of IPv6-native national digital content, including cultural, educational, and commercial services, reducing reliance on foreign platforms.
  4. Long-Term Resilience Strategy: Develop a comprehensive national internet resilience strategy that integrates IPv6, RPKI, DNSSEC, and other advanced security protocols as core tenets of digital sovereignty and national security.
