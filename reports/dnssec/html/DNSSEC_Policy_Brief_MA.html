Executive summary

Morocco's DNS ecosystem demonstrates a foundational level of infrastructure resilience, particularly in the authoritative nameserver setup for the .ma ccTLD. The eight authoritative nameservers for .ma exhibit strong IPv6 enablement at 75.0% and are distributed across six distinct Autonomous Systems, enhancing resilience against single points of failure. This architectural strength provides a solid basis for DNS operations. However, this robust infrastructure capability contrasts with the operational deployment and validation rates observed in the broader DNS landscape.

Despite the technical readiness, the overall DNSSEC validation rate for observed queries in Morocco stands at 22.70%, with a significant 75.81% of queries being insecure. This indicates that while the infrastructure may support DNSSEC, its actual implementation and validation by local resolvers and networks are critically underutilized. A notable disparity exists across major networks, with secure validation percentages ranging from 4.05% to 40.76%. Furthermore, only 0.60% of DNS traffic utilizes IPv6, a stark contrast to the 75.0% IPv6 enablement of the .ma ccTLD nameservers. This highlights a disconnect between core infrastructure readiness and end-user adoption and resolver configuration. The concentration of DNS traffic on global top-level domains, with .ma accounting for only 0.35% of queries, further emphasizes the need for local content development and security enhancements. Addressing these operational gaps, particularly in DNSSEC resolver validation and IPv6 adoption, is crucial for improving Morocco's internet resilience.

1. Where Morocco stands

Morocco's DNS infrastructure for its .ma ccTLD demonstrates robust design, particularly in its authoritative nameserver setup. The deployment of eight authoritative nameservers, with an impressive 75.0% IPv6 enablement, positions Morocco favorably in terms of foundational resilience and future readiness compared to many countries that still struggle with basic IPv6 support for critical infrastructure. Furthermore, the distribution of these nameservers across six distinct Autonomous Systems indicates a proactive approach to mitigating network and routing failure risks, exceeding the minimum recommendations for geographical and network diversity. However, these architectural strengths are not yet fully leveraged across the broader national DNS ecosystem.

Two readings matter for policy. First, the low overall DNSSEC secure validation rate of 22.70% for observed queries, coupled with a dominant 75.81% insecure query share, reveals a significant gap between the country's infrastructure capability and its operational security posture. This indicates that while the core DNS system may be ready, most end-users' DNS resolvers are not effectively validating DNSSEC, leaving a substantial portion of DNS traffic vulnerable. Second, the discrepancy between the 75.0% IPv6 enablement for .ma nameservers and the mere 0.60% share of IPv6 in observed DNS traffic highlights a critical last-mile adoption challenge. This suggests that even with a strong authoritative foundation, the absence of widespread IPv6 connectivity and resolver configuration among ISPs and end-users prevents the full realization of its benefits, impacting both efficiency and future internet growth.

With eight nameservers, the country ensures adequate redundancy and capacity to handle DNS queries for its national domain. This robust provisioning helps safeguard against outages and ensures continuous availability of .ma domains, providing a stable foundation for the digital economy.

The distribution of these nameservers across six distinct Autonomous Systems significantly enhances the resilience of the .ma ccTLD. This multi-ASN deployment strategy mitigates the risk of a single point of failure related to an ASN-specific outage or routing incident, ensuring that DNS resolution for .ma remains operational even if one network experiences issues. Such diversity is critical for maintaining high availability and trust in the national domain.

3. Where Morocco could improve

Morocco's internet resilience could be significantly enhanced by addressing critical gaps in DNSSEC validation, IPv6 adoption, and granular DNSSEC performance across networks, which currently undermine the country's otherwise robust ccTLD infrastructure.

3.1 Low Overall DNSSEC Validation

A primary area for improvement is the low overall DNSSEC secure validation rate. With only 22.70% of observed DNS queries resolved to DNSSEC-signed domains and validated as secure, a substantial majority of DNS traffic in Morocco (75.81%) remains insecure. This indicates that most recursive DNS resolvers within the country are either not configured to perform DNSSEC validation or are not actively validating, leaving users vulnerable to DNS spoofing and cache poisoning attacks.

The absence of widespread DNSSEC validation undermines the integrity and authenticity of domain name lookups, despite the potential for domains to be signed. This situation creates a significant security vulnerability for internet users, as malicious actors could redirect traffic to fraudulent websites or intercept sensitive information by manipulating DNS responses. Prioritizing the activation and enforcement of DNSSEC validation across all major network operators is crucial for enhancing national cybersecurity.

3.2 Disparate DNSSEC Validation Across ASNs

The wide variance in DNSSEC secure validation rates across different Autonomous Systems (ASNs) operating in Morocco points to an inconsistent approach to DNS security. While some networks, such as AS36903 (Office National des Postes et Telecommunications ONPT), achieve a respectable 40.76% secure validation, others like AS14593 (Space Exploration Technologies Corporation) lag significantly with only 4.05%. This disparity indicates a lack of uniform best practices or policy enforcement regarding DNSSEC.

This uneven adoption creates a fragmented security landscape where users on certain networks are far more exposed to DNS-related threats than others. The lower-performing ASNs contribute disproportionately to the overall low national secure validation rate, highlighting specific targets for intervention. Engaging these networks to understand and overcome their barriers to DNSSEC implementation is essential for raising the national average and ensuring a more equitable level of security for all internet users in Morocco.

3.3 Critically Low IPv6 DNS Traffic

Despite 75.0% of .ma ccTLD authoritative nameservers supporting IPv6, only 0.60% of observed DNS traffic in Morocco utilizes IPv6. This significant disparity reveals a critical gap in end-to-end IPv6 deployment and adoption within the country. The readiness of core infrastructure is not matched by the operational use of IPv6 by end-users or their recursive DNS resolvers.

This low IPv6 traffic share indicates that most internet users and services in Morocco are still predominantly operating on IPv4. This limits the long-term scalability and resilience of the internet ecosystem, as IPv4 addresses become increasingly scarce and network operators cannot fully leverage the benefits of IPv6, such as improved routing efficiency and increased address space for connected devices. Accelerating IPv6 deployment at the access network and resolver levels is crucial for future-proofing Morocco's internet.

6. Policy gaps and loopholes

6.1 Where policy is absent or unenforced

Gap Consequence Remedy
Lack of clear mandate for DNSSEC validation Low and inconsistent DNSSEC secure validation rates across ASNs, leaving users vulnerable to DNS spoofing. Implement a regulatory directive requiring all ISPs to enable DNSSEC validation on their recursive resolvers.
Absence of IPv6 adoption incentives Critically low IPv6 DNS traffic despite infrastructure readiness, hindering long-term internet growth and efficiency. Introduce policy incentives (e.g., tax breaks, grants) for ISPs and content providers to accelerate IPv6 deployment.
No specific policy on DNSSEC signing for critical national infrastructure and government domains Key national digital assets remain unprotected against DNS tampering. Mandate DNSSEC signing for all government and critical infrastructure .ma domains.
Insufficient monitoring and reporting requirements for DNS security metrics Difficulty in tracking progress, identifying underperforming networks, and adapting policies effectively. Require ISPs to regularly report DNSSEC validation rates and IPv6 traffic statistics to the regulator.

6.2 Loopholes to avoid creating

  • Mandating specific DNS resolver software or infrastructure, which could stifle innovation and competition.
  • Imposing overly prescriptive technical standards that do not allow for operational flexibility or emerging technologies.
  • Creating financial burdens on smaller operators without corresponding support mechanisms, potentially leading to market consolidation or reduced service quality.
  • Establishing a single point of authority for DNSSEC key management, which could introduce a centralized vulnerability.
  • Implementing policies that disincentivize or penalize the use of public, secure DNS resolvers (e.g., 1.1.1.1, 8.8.8.8) which often offer higher security standards.

Conclusion

Morocco's foundational internet infrastructure for the .ma ccTLD demonstrates commendable resilience, notably through its diversified and IPv6-enabled authoritative nameserver setup. This technical readiness presents a significant opportunity for the country to leapfrog in internet security and performance. However, a critical disconnect persists between this physical network presence and the operational deployment of key security configurations, particularly DNSSEC validation and widespread IPv6 adoption in daily DNS traffic. The low overall secure validation rate and the wide variance in DNSSEC performance among networks highlight a pressing need for concerted action to bridge this gap.

By prioritizing cost-effective configuration changes and administrative coordination efforts in the short term, Morocco can rapidly accelerate its DNSSEC validation rates and boost IPv6 adoption without immediate capital-intensive investments. These quick wins will not only enhance the immediate security posture of the national internet but also build crucial regulatory momentum and technical expertise. This phased approach, starting with strategic administrative and policy interventions before moving to larger structural upgrades, ensures that progress is sustainable, efficient, and ultimately delivers a more secure and resilient internet experience for all users in Morocco.
