Executive summary

France's .fr ccTLD infrastructure demonstrates robust foundational elements for resilience, with all 3 authoritative nameservers fully supporting IPv6 connectivity and distributed across 4 distinct Autonomous Systems. This technical capability indicates a strong underlying network architecture for the country's top-level domain. However, this infrastructural strength currently contrasts with the operational deployment of DNSSEC validation across the national ecosystem, indicating a gap between capability and widespread implementation.

Only 9.98% of observed DNS queries in France resolve to DNSSEC-signed domains and are successfully validated (SECURE), while a substantial 84.05% query unsigned domains (INSECURE). This overall figure masks significant disparities among major network operators, with secure validation rates ranging from a low of 2.05% for Datacamp Limited to a high of 19.84% for Bouygues Telecom. The national .fr ccTLD itself accounts for a minor share (2.16%) of overall DNS traffic, with global TLDs like .com dominating at 59.15%. Furthermore, despite 100% IPv6 readiness at the .fr ccTLD authoritative level, IPv6 only accounts for 7.28% of observed DNS queries, indicating that infrastructure readiness currently exceeds end-user adoption. These combined factors suggest a critical need for coordinated efforts to drive wider DNSSEC validation and IPv6 adoption among Internet Service Providers (ISPs) and across the national user base.

1. Where France stands

France currently presents a paradox in its DNS security posture. While the foundational infrastructure for the .fr ccTLD demonstrates strong technical resilience, with 100% IPv6 enablement for its authoritative nameservers and a distributed architecture across multiple Autonomous Systems, the operational deployment of DNSSEC validation across the broader ecosystem remains significantly underdeveloped. This contrast indicates that while the technical capability for a secure DNS environment exists at the core, its benefits are not yet widely extended to end-users and the broader internet community within the country.

Two readings matter for policy. First, the robust and diversified infrastructure for .fr, coupled with stable DNS resolution health, signals a high level of preparedness and operational maturity for the national top-level domain. This existing strength can serve as a launchpad for broader security initiatives. Second, the low overall DNSSEC validation rate of 9.98%, combined with significant disparities among major network operators, highlights a critical operational gap. Addressing this gap requires targeted policy intervention and concerted engagement with ISPs to ensure that the security benefits of DNSSEC are uniformly delivered to internet users across France.

2. Where France is improving

2.1 Robust ccTLD Authoritative Infrastructure

The authoritative DNS infrastructure for the .fr ccTLD exhibits strong technical resilience and forward-looking adoption of modern internet protocols. All three authoritative nameservers for .fr are fully enabled with IPv6, achieving a 100% IPv6 enablement rate. This comprehensive support ensures that the national domain can be resolved efficiently over both IPv4 and IPv6 networks, contributing to overall network accessibility and performance.

Further enhancing resilience, these authoritative nameservers are distributed across 4 distinct Autonomous Systems. This diversity mitigates single points of failure, protecting against potential outages or routing incidents that could affect individual networks. By spreading critical DNS functions across multiple independent ASNs, the .fr ccTLD maintains a robust and highly available resolution service, a key factor in national internet stability.

2.2 Stable DNS Resolution Health

France's DNS ecosystem demonstrates a high level of operational stability, as evidenced by its robust DNS resolution health metrics. Approximately 81.37% of all DNS queries observed successfully completed with a NOERROR response, indicating reliable and accurate resolution. Critically, the rate of SERVFAIL responses remained low, at under 2%, which is a strong indicator of well-maintained and efficiently operating DNS infrastructure across the country.

Furthermore, the observed DNS cache behavior shows that nearly 75.58% of DNS queries were answered using positive cache entries. This high positive cache rate suggests that resolvers are efficiently caching responses, which reduces upstream query load and improves resolution latency for end-users. Such stable and efficient resolution contributes positively to the overall user experience and the foundational reliability of internet access in France.

3. Where France could improve

France could significantly enhance its internet resilience by addressing the low overall DNSSEC validation, disparate operator adoption, and limited IPv6 query deployment.

3.1 Low DNSSEC Validation Across the Ecosystem

A primary area for improvement in France's DNS security posture is the overall low rate of DNSSEC validation. Only 9.98% of observed DNS queries are securely validated, while a significant 84.05% pertain to insecure, unsigned domains. This indicates that the vast majority of DNS traffic in France is not protected by DNSSEC, leaving users vulnerable to DNS spoofing and cache poisoning attacks. The small fraction of "Invalid" responses (0.04%) suggests that while errors in DNSSEC chains are rare, the sheer volume of unsigned or unvalidated queries represents a systemic security vulnerability.

This widespread lack of DNSSEC validation means that the integrity and authenticity of DNS responses for most internet traffic cannot be cryptographically verified. While some domains may not be signed at their origin, a greater concern is the low validation rate for those that are. Enhancing DNSSEC validation would significantly bolster the trustworthiness of DNS resolution across the country, making it more resilient against manipulation and enhancing user confidence in online services.

3.2 Inconsistent DNSSEC Adoption Among Major ASNs

The deployment of DNSSEC validation varies considerably across major Autonomous Systems (ASNs) operating in France, highlighting an inconsistent approach to DNS security among network operators. While Bouygues Telecom (AS5410) demonstrates leadership with 19.84% secure validation and Orange France (AS3215) and SFR Group (AS15557) also show strong efforts at 16.09% and 16.61% respectively, other significant players lag considerably. For instance, Datacamp Limited (AS212238) registers only 2.05% secure validation, and Contabo GmbH (AS51167) stands at 4.57%.

This wide disparity in secure validation rates suggests a fragmented security landscape where the level of protection users receive depends heavily on their internet service provider. Networks with low validation rates not only expose their own users to greater risks but also diminish the overall security posture of the national internet ecosystem. Targeted engagement and potential policy incentives are necessary to encourage these lower-performing ASNs to prioritize and implement DNSSEC validation more broadly, aiming for a more uniform and robust security baseline across all networks.

3.3 Underutilization of IPv6 for DNS Queries

Despite the .fr ccTLD authoritative nameservers fully supporting IPv6, the observed share of DNS queries utilizing IPv6 in France remains low at 7.28%, with IPv4 dominating at 92.72%. This disparity indicates a significant gap between the readiness of core internet infrastructure and its adoption by end-users and their local DNS resolvers. The underutilization of IPv6 for DNS queries represents a missed opportunity to leverage the benefits of the newer protocol, including potentially improved performance, enhanced routing efficiency, and greater network address availability.

A higher IPv6 query share would contribute to a more modern and resilient internet ecosystem, reducing reliance on the increasingly congested IPv4 address space. Promoting broader IPv6 deployment among ISPs and encouraging the configuration of resolvers to prefer IPv6 where available would allow France to fully capitalize on its already advanced ccTLD infrastructure and ensure a more future-proof internet experience for its citizens.

4. A staged plan to improve resilience

Improving France's internet resilience, particularly regarding DNSSEC validation and IPv6 adoption, necessitates a phased approach. Phase 1 focuses on immediate, low-cost configuration and administrative actions achievable within 12 months. Phase 2 involves targeted coordination and incentivization over 1-3 years. Phase 3 aims for long-term structural capacity building, spanning 3-7 years, to embed these security and protocol enhancements deeply into the national internet fabric.

6. Policy gaps and loopholes

6.1 Where policy is absent or unenforced

Gap Consequence Remedy
Lack of mandatory DNSSEC validation for government and critical infrastructure domains. Public sector and vital services remain vulnerable to DNS spoofing, undermining public trust and national security. Mandate DNSSEC validation for all government domains and critical national infrastructure providers, enforced through procurement or regulatory compliance.
Absence of incentives for Internet Service Providers (ISPs) to deploy DNSSEC validation by default. Stagnant adoption rates for DNSSEC validation across the broader user base, leading to a fragmented and less secure national internet. Introduce regulatory or economic incentives for ISPs that implement DNSSEC validation by default for their subscribers.
No explicit national strategy or coordination body specifically for DNS security. Disjointed efforts and a lack of unified direction in advancing DNSSEC adoption and other DNS security measures. Establish a dedicated national multi-stakeholder working group or integrate DNS security as a specific pillar within the national cybersecurity strategy.

6.2 Loopholes to avoid creating

  • Mandating specific DNS resolver hardware or software, which stifles innovation and creates vendor lock-in.
  • Imposing blanket DNS blocking policies that lack transparent oversight and due process, potentially leading to censorship or over-blocking.
  • Creating national-only DNS resolvers that could fragment the global DNS and introduce single points of failure or surveillance.
  • Requiring client-side DNSSEC validation without sufficient server-side support, leading to user experience issues and unnecessary complexity.
  • Implementing policies that force all DNS traffic through a limited set of national providers, increasing centralization and reducing resilience.

Conclusion

France possesses a robust and resilient foundational DNS infrastructure for its .fr ccTLD, characterized by 100% IPv6 enablement for its authoritative nameservers and strong ASN diversity. This technical capability, however, currently stands in disconnect with the operational deployment of DNSSEC validation across the broader national ecosystem. The low overall secure validation rate of 9.98% and significant disparities among network operators indicate a substantial gap between inherent capacity and widespread security implementation, leaving the majority of DNS traffic vulnerable to known threats.

To effectively bridge this gap and enhance national internet resilience, it is crucial to sequence strategic interventions, prioritizing immediate, low-cost configuration and administrative "quick wins" before embarking on capital-heavy structural projects. Initiatives such as targeted awareness campaigns, direct engagement with underperforming ASNs, and the promotion of IPv6 on resolver infrastructure can build significant momentum. This approach will demonstrate tangible progress, generate buy-in from stakeholders, and pave the way for more comprehensive policy frameworks and long-term investments needed to secure France's digital future.
