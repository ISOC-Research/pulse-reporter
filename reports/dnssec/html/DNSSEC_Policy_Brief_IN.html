Executive summary

India exhibits a notable dichotomy in its DNSSEC posture: while the foundational infrastructure for the .in country code Top-Level Domain (ccTLD) demonstrates robust security readiness, the actual operational deployment of DNSSEC validation at the resolver level remains significantly underutilised. The .in ccTLD benefits from strong resilience, with all four authoritative nameservers supporting IPv6 and distributed across three distinct Autonomous Systems. This indicates a high degree of technical capability at the registry level to secure the national domain space. However, this infrastructural strength does not yet translate into widespread end-user protection, as only 9.16% of DNS queries observed via public resolvers are securely validated with DNSSEC, falling substantially short of international best practice targets.

A detailed examination reveals that 86.75% of observed queries resolve to insecure domains, highlighting a significant gap in the protection of Indian internet users against DNS spoofing and cache poisoning attacks. Furthermore, the rate of DNSSEC-secured query validation varies widely across major domestic networks, ranging from a low of 3.90% for one prominent provider to a high of 18.94% for another. This inconsistency suggests a lack of uniform DNSSEC implementation strategies among Internet Service Providers (ISPs) and network operators. Despite the 100% IPv6 enablement of .in ccTLD nameservers, IPv6 accounts for only 10.88% of overall DNS traffic, indicating that infrastructure readiness currently outpaces IPv6 adoption at the end-user and resolver level. These factors collectively underscore an urgent need for concerted efforts to translate existing technical capacity into broader operational security and user protection.

1. Where India stands

India's DNSSEC landscape presents a paradox of strong foundational capability versus nascent operational deployment. The country's .in ccTLD demonstrates excellent readiness with all four authoritative nameservers fully supporting IPv6 and distributed across three distinct Autonomous Systems, a configuration that significantly enhances resilience against network failures and routing incidents. This robust backend infrastructure signifies a proactive stance towards securing the national domain space at the registry level, aligning with global best practices for critical internet infrastructure. However, this robust foundation has not yet translated into widespread end-user protection, as evidenced by a low DNSSEC secure validation rate.

The current secure validation rate for DNS queries in India stands at a mere 9.16%, meaning the vast majority of DNS resolutions are not benefiting from the cryptographic protections offered by DNSSEC. This figure places India significantly below international benchmarks and the recommended target for widespread DNSSEC adoption. The disparity between the ccTLD's technical preparedness and the low validation rates among resolvers indicates a critical gap in the security chain, leaving end-users vulnerable to DNS manipulation attacks.

Two readings matter for policy. First, the 100% IPv6 enablement and diverse ASN distribution of the .in ccTLD authoritative nameservers confirm that the technical capacity for secure DNS operations is firmly established at the national domain registry level. This eliminates a major potential barrier to broader DNSSEC adoption. Second, the significantly low and widely varying DNSSEC secure validation rates across domestic networks, with a national average of 9.16%, highlight a clear operational deficit at the ISP and resolver level. This indicates that efforts must now focus on incentivising and enabling network operators to deploy and enforce DNSSEC validation for their users.

2. Where India is improving

2.1 ccTLD Authoritative Nameserver Resilience

India has made commendable progress in ensuring the resilience and modernity of its .in ccTLD authoritative nameserver infrastructure. All four authoritative nameservers for the .in ccTLD are fully IPv6 enabled, achieving a 100% enablement rate. This comprehensive support for IPv6 not only aligns with contemporary internet standards but also future-proofs the national domain's core services, enhancing accessibility and efficiency for users on IPv6-only networks.

Beyond IPv6 enablement, the .in ccTLD nameservers are strategically distributed across three distinct Autonomous Systems. This diversity is crucial for resilience, as it mitigates risks associated with single points of failure, such as routing issues, network outages, or targeted attacks against a specific network operator. Such a distributed architecture ensures continuous availability and reliability of critical DNS services for the national domain, a fundamental component of internet stability.

2.2 Stable DNS Resolution Health

The overall health of DNS resolution within India's internet ecosystem is robust, with a high success rate for DNS queries. Approximately 87.05% of observed DNS queries successfully completed with a NOERROR response code, indicating that the vast majority of requests are processed without issues and domains are resolved correctly. This strong performance in query resolution is a foundational element for a reliable and functional internet experience.

Furthermore, SERVFAIL responses, which indicate server-side errors during resolution, remained low at 2.05%. This low error rate suggests that DNS infrastructure components, including resolvers and authoritative servers, are largely stable and well-maintained. A healthy DNS resolution environment provides a solid platform upon which more advanced security protocols like DNSSEC can be effectively deployed and integrated.

3. Where India could improve

India's internet ecosystem faces key challenges in operational DNSSEC deployment, inconsistent operator validation, and underutilised IPv6 for DNS traffic, each imposing varying costs on national internet resilience.

3.1 Low DNSSEC Resolver Validation

Despite the strong foundational readiness of the .in ccTLD infrastructure for DNSSEC, the actual validation of DNSSEC-signed domains by resolvers in India remains critically low. Only 9.16% of all observed DNS queries are securely validated using DNSSEC, while a substantial 86.75% resolve to insecure domains. This pronounced gap indicates that the benefits of DNSSEC, such as protection against DNS spoofing and cache poisoning, are not reaching the majority of Indian internet users.

The low adoption rate of DNSSEC validation at the resolver level represents a significant security vulnerability for the country's internet users. Without DNSSEC validation, users are more susceptible to malicious actors redirecting their traffic to fraudulent websites or intercepting sensitive information. Addressing this gap requires a concerted effort to encourage and enable ISPs and network operators to implement and enforce DNSSEC validation for their customer base.

3.2 Inconsistent DNSSEC Validation Across Networks

The analysis of DNSSEC validation across different Autonomous Systems in India reveals significant inconsistencies in implementation and adoption. While some networks, such as Bharti Airtel Limited (AS9498), achieve a secure validation rate of 18.94%, others, including the National Internet Backbone (AS9829), record as low as 3.90%. This wide disparity indicates a lack of a unified approach or policy regarding DNSSEC deployment among network operators.

Such inconsistencies mean that the level of DNS security experienced by users is highly dependent on their chosen ISP, leading to an uneven security posture across the country. Networks with low validation rates contribute disproportionately to the overall low national average and expose a large segment of users to elevated risks. Targeted engagement with these lower-performing networks is essential to elevate the national average and ensure a more uniform level of DNS security.

3.3 Underutilised IPv6 for DNS Traffic

Despite the .in ccTLD's authoritative nameservers fully supporting IPv6, the observed share of DNS traffic utilising IPv6 remains low at only 10.88%. This indicates a significant discrepancy between the readiness of critical infrastructure and its actual operational adoption within the broader internet ecosystem. The majority of DNS queries (89.12%) still rely on IPv4, limiting the benefits of IPv6's expanded address space and potentially improved routing efficiency.

The low IPv6 DNS traffic share suggests that while the foundational elements for a next-generation internet are in place at the ccTLD level, the transition to IPv6 is not yet pervasive among end-users, ISPs, and content providers. Accelerating IPv6 adoption, particularly for DNS resolvers, is crucial for improving overall network resilience, performance, and preparing for future internet growth. This also has implications for DNSSEC, as IPv6-enabled resolvers can more efficiently process DNSSEC data over IPv6 paths.

6. Policy gaps and loopholes

6.1 Where policy is absent or unenforced

Gap Consequence Remedy
Lack of mandate for DNSSEC validation by ISPs Low overall DNSSEC secure validation rates across the country, leaving users vulnerable to DNS attacks. Introduce a policy recommendation or phased mandate for all ISPs to enable DNSSEC validation on their recursive resolvers.
Absence of incentives for .in domain DNSSEC signing Slow adoption of DNSSEC by .in domain holders, despite the ccTLD's foundational readiness. Implement registration fee discounts or public recognition programs for DNSSEC-signed .in domains.
No national guidelines for DNSSEC deployment for public sector Inconsistent or non-existent DNSSEC protection for critical government and public service websites. Develop and enforce specific DNSSEC implementation guidelines for all government and critical infrastructure domain owners.
Limited technical training and support for operators Operators may lack the knowledge or resources to properly deploy and maintain DNSSEC. Fund and promote national DNSSEC training programs and technical assistance initiatives for ISPs and domain administrators.

6.2 Loopholes to avoid creating

  • Avoid mandating specific DNSSEC software or vendor solutions, which can lead to vendor lock-in and stifle innovation.
  • Refrain from imposing overly complex or bureaucratic DNSSEC implementation processes that might discourage adoption, particularly for smaller entities.
  • Do not create policies that centralize DNSSEC key management in a single entity, as this could introduce a critical single point of failure.
  • Avoid setting unrealistic timelines for DNSSEC deployment, especially for smaller operators, without providing adequate support or resources.
  • Ensure that any mandates are technology-neutral and focus on security outcomes rather than prescriptive technical methods.

Conclusion

India's current DNSSEC posture reveals a significant disconnect between the readiness of its core national domain infrastructure and the operational security provided to its internet users. While the .in ccTLD demonstrates strong technical capabilities with 100% IPv6 enablement and diversified authoritative nameservers, the broader ecosystem suffers from extremely low DNSSEC validation rates among resolvers and inconsistent deployment across network operators. This creates a critical vulnerability, exposing the majority of Indian internet traffic to the risks of DNS manipulation attacks, despite the underlying capacity to prevent them.

Addressing this gap requires a strategic sequence of interventions, prioritising configuration and administrative changes that yield quick security wins before embarking on more capital-intensive projects. Encouraging ISPs to enable DNSSEC validation, publishing clear deployment guidelines, and engaging directly with underperforming networks represent low-effort, high-impact actions. Successfully implementing these "quick wins" can build crucial regulatory momentum and demonstrate tangible improvements in security posture, paving the way for medium- to long-term policies such as mandating DNSSEC for critical domains and investing in broader infrastructure upgrades. This phased approach will systematically enhance India's DNSSEC resilience, translating technical capability into widespread user protection.
