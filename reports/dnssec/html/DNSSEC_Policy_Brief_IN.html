Executive summary

India presents a contrasting landscape in its DNSSEC deployment. While the foundational infrastructure for the national .in ccTLD demonstrates robust security and resilience, the operational deployment of DNSSEC validation across the broader internet ecosystem remains significantly underdeveloped. The .in ccTLD is served by 4 authoritative nameservers, all of which are IPv6 enabled, achieving a 100.0% enablement rate. These nameservers are strategically distributed across 3 distinct Autonomous Systems, enhancing resilience against single points of failure. This architectural strength at the national registry level provides a solid foundation for DNSSEC adoption, positioning India favorably in terms of core domain security capabilities.

Despite this strong infrastructure, actual DNSSEC secure validation at the resolver level is markedly low, with only 9.18% of observed DNS queries being validated as SECURE. Conversely, 87.08% of queries resolve to unsigned domains (INSECURE), indicating a widespread lack of DNSSEC implementation or validation by network operators. Significant disparities exist in validation rates across major networks, ranging from a low of 3.87% to a high of 19.01%. Furthermore, the national .in ccTLD accounts for only 2.39% of total DNS queries, and IPv6 traffic constitutes merely 10.90% of DNS queries, suggesting that while the technical capacity for a secure and modern internet exists at the ccTLD level, its benefits are not yet fully leveraged by the end-user ecosystem. This gap between readiness and active deployment underscores the need for targeted interventions to drive broader DNSSEC adoption and validation.

1. Where India stands

India's DNSSEC posture is characterised by a notable divergence between its foundational capabilities and operational realities. The nation's .in ccTLD infrastructure is robust, with all 4 authoritative nameservers supporting IPv6 and distributed across 3 distinct Autonomous Systems, fulfilling best practices for resilience and modern internet protocols. This robust backend infrastructure provides a solid technical base for securing domain name resolution. However, the broader ecosystem lags significantly in adopting DNSSEC validation. Only 9.18% of DNS queries observed from India's networks are securely validated, falling critically short of optimal security targets. This figure places India far behind regions and countries with comprehensive DNSSEC adoption strategies, where secure validation rates often exceed 50-70% and aim for 100%.

Two readings matter for policy. First, the excellent technical health of the .in ccTLD's authoritative DNS infrastructure demonstrates national competence in deploying critical internet security mechanisms, indicating that the technical expertise for DNSSEC exists within the country. Second, the alarmingly low rate of secure DNSSEC validation by end-user resolvers, coupled with significant disparities across major networks, points to a clear policy and implementation gap. This indicates that despite the availability of secure infrastructure, network operators and internet service providers (ISPs) have not universally enabled DNSSEC validation, leaving a vast majority of users vulnerable to DNS-related attacks.

2. Where India is improving

2.1 Robust .in ccTLD Infrastructure and IPv6 Enablement

The .in ccTLD demonstrates strong foundational health, with its authoritative DNS infrastructure fully adhering to modern internet standards. All 4 authoritative nameservers for the .in domain are IPv6 enabled, achieving a 100.0% enablement rate. This comprehensive support for IPv6 ensures that the national domain can be resolved efficiently and securely over both IPv4 and IPv6 networks, providing redundancy and future-proofing the core domain infrastructure.

The proactive deployment of IPv6 across all authoritative nameservers for the .in ccTLD is a significant positive indicator. It reflects a commitment to advanced internet protocols at the national registry level. This complete IPv6 readiness stands as a robust asset, ready to support increasing IPv6 adoption rates among end-users and network operators. This technical leadership from the ccTLD registry provides a strong example for other critical internet infrastructure providers within India.

2.2 Authoritative Nameserver ASN Diversity

The authoritative DNS infrastructure for the .in ccTLD exhibits commendable resilience through its distribution across multiple Autonomous Systems (ASNs). The 4 authoritative nameservers are hosted across 3 distinct ASNs, which significantly mitigates the risk of single-point failures. This diversity ensures that resolution for .in domains remains operational even in the event of an outage or routing issue affecting a single network provider.

This strategic distribution across ASNs is a critical best practice for maintaining the availability and integrity of a country code Top-Level Domain. It ensures robust redundancy and protects against various network-centric threats, including DDoS attacks or routing incidents specific to a single network operator. Such diversity enhances the overall security posture of the .in domain, creating a more stable and reliable foundation for internet users in India.

3. Where India could improve

India's internet resilience could be significantly enhanced by addressing its low DNSSEC validation rates, inconsistent operator performance, and underutilised IPv6 potential, each of which contributes to a less secure and less efficient national internet experience.

3.1 Low DNSSEC Secure Validation Rate

A primary area for improvement is the alarmingly low rate of DNSSEC secure validation across India's networks. Only 9.18% of all observed DNS queries are securely validated, meaning the vast majority of DNS resolutions are vulnerable to manipulation and spoofing. This exposes internet users to risks such as phishing, malware distribution, and man-in-the-middle attacks, undermining trust in the internet infrastructure.

The high percentage of insecure queries (87.08%) indicates that most internet service providers and network operators have not enabled DNSSEC validation on their recursive resolvers. This fundamental oversight negates the security benefits of DNSSEC, even for domains that are properly signed. Addressing this requires widespread adoption of validating resolvers across the national network ecosystem.

3.2 Inconsistent DNSSEC Validation Across Major Networks

The observed DNSSEC validation rates vary significantly among major network operators in India, highlighting a fragmented approach to DNS security. While some networks, such as Bharti Airtel Limited (AS9498), show a relatively higher secure validation rate of 19.01%, others, like the National Internet Backbone (AS9829), exhibit a critically low rate of 3.87% despite accounting for a significant share of DNS traffic (15.93%).

Large networks with substantial user bases and low validation rates pose a disproportionately higher risk to the overall security of the Indian internet. A concerted effort to bring all major operators up to a higher baseline is essential to ensure consistent protection for all internet users.

3.3 Low .in ccTLD Query Share and IPv6 Traffic Adoption

Despite the .in ccTLD's robust and IPv6-enabled authoritative infrastructure, its share of DNS queries remains low at 2.39%. This suggests that local content and services are not predominantly using the national domain, potentially limiting the direct impact of the ccTLD's security enhancements on the broader internet experience for Indian users. Furthermore, only 10.90% of observed DNS traffic uses IPv6, which is significantly lower than the 100% IPv6 enablement rate of the .in ccTLD nameservers.

While the ccTLD is prepared for the next generation of the internet, the ecosystem's slow transition means that most users are not benefiting from the efficiencies and potential security enhancements that IPv6 offers. Efforts are needed to encourage both greater adoption of the .in ccTLD for local services and accelerated deployment of IPv6 by ISPs and businesses.

6. Policy gaps and loopholes

6.1 Where policy is absent or unenforced

Gap Consequence Remedy
Lack of mandatory DNSSEC validation for ISPs Widespread insecure DNS resolution, leaving users vulnerable to DNS spoofing and cache poisoning. Introduce regulatory mandates for all licensed ISPs to enable DNSSEC validation on their recursive resolvers.
Insufficient incentives for .in domain holders to sign their domains Limited number of .in domains are DNSSEC-signed, reducing the overall security of the national domain space. Implement incentive programs or policy guidance for registrars to promote and simplify DNSSEC signing for .in domains.
Absence of a comprehensive national strategy for DNSSEC adoption Fragmented and inconsistent deployment of DNSSEC across different networks and government entities. Develop and integrate a formal national DNSSEC adoption strategy within India's cybersecurity framework.
Low public and enterprise awareness of DNSSEC benefits Lack of demand from end-users and businesses for DNSSEC-secured services, reducing pressure on operators to implement it. Launch broad national awareness campaigns targeting both technical and non-technical audiences on the importance of DNSSEC.

6.2 Loopholes to avoid creating

  • Mandating specific DNSSEC validating resolver software or hardware, which could stifle innovation and competition among service providers.
  • Implementing overly complex or costly DNSSEC signing processes for .in domain holders, discouraging adoption.
  • Allowing a "whitelist" approach to DNSSEC where only pre-approved domains are validated, rather than a universal validation of all signed domains.
  • Introducing policies that inadvertently favour large operators at the expense of smaller, regional ISPs, creating further market concentration.
  • Automatically blocking or quarantining unsigned domains without clear policy justification, which could disrupt legitimate services.

Conclusion

India's DNSSEC landscape presents a classic disconnect between physical network presence and operational security configuration. While the .in ccTLD boasts a robust and resilient authoritative DNS infrastructure with full IPv6 enablement and diverse ASN distribution, the actual secure validation of DNS queries by end-users remains critically low. This paradoxical situation means that a strong foundation has been laid, yet the final crucial step of enabling validation at the resolver level—the primary mechanism for users to benefit from DNSSEC's security—is largely overlooked. The wide variance in validation rates across major operators further underscores this uneven operational maturity, highlighting specific bottlenecks that require targeted intervention.

To overcome this, a strategic sequencing of interventions is crucial. Prioritising immediate, configuration-based "quick wins" for DNSSEC validation across major ISPs, coupled with awareness campaigns and simplified registry processes, can yield rapid, measurable improvements without significant capital outlay. These initial successes can then build regulatory momentum and industry confidence, paving the way for more comprehensive policy mandates, stronger incentives for domain signing, and long-term integration of DNSSEC into the national cybersecurity strategy. By systematically addressing the operational gaps and capitalising on the existing strong ccTLD infrastructure, India can significantly enhance its internet resilience and provide a more secure online environment for its citizens and businesses.
