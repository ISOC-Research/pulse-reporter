Executive summary

India’s DNS ecosystem presents a significant structural paradox where high resolution stability coexists with low cryptographic security adoption. While 85.63% of DNS queries are resolved successfully, the national DNSSEC validation rate stands at 10.12%. This indicates that nearly 90% of Internet traffic in India remains vulnerable to DNS hijacking and cache poisoning attacks. The digital landscape is heavily dominated by global Top-Level Domains (TLDs), with .com accounting for 67.46% of traffic, dwarfing the national .in ccTLD which captures only 2.24% of the query share.

Technical metrics reveal substantial inconsistencies across major network providers. Secure validation rates range from a high of 17.21% in Bharti Airtel (AS24560) to a low of 5.03% in the National Internet Backbone (AS9829). Furthermore, despite the availability of IPv6 infrastructure, only 10.32% of DNS traffic utilizes IPv6 transport, suggesting a bottleneck at the user-edge or within recursive resolver configurations. The authoritative infrastructure for the .in ccTLD currently lacks visible IPv6 enablement and ASN diversity in the assessed dataset, representing a critical area for resilience benchmarking.

1. Where India stands

India’s DNSSEC adoption is currently in a nascent stage compared to global leaders in secure DNS infrastructure. With only 10.12% of queries undergoing secure validation, the country trails significantly in protecting its users from man-in-the-middle attacks at the DNS layer. The distribution of validation across Autonomous Systems shows that even the most advanced networks in India have not yet crossed the 20% threshold for secure validation.

Two readings matter for policy. First, the disparity between the highest-performing network (17.21%) and the largest public sector backbone (5.03%) indicates a lack of unified security standards or regulatory mandates for DNSSEC. Second, the overwhelming preference for .com (67.46%) over .in (2.24%) suggests that security improvements in the .in zone alone will have limited impact on the total user base unless recursive resolvers are configured to validate all signed zones globally.

2. Where India is improving

2.1 High operational stability and resolution success

The DNS ecosystem in India demonstrates high levels of operational reliability. A NOERROR response rate of 85.63% indicates that the underlying network routing and nameserver availability are stable. Furthermore, the low SERVFAIL rate (2.10%) suggests that even where DNSSEC is implemented, it is not currently causing widespread resolution failures due to misconfiguration.

2.2 Emerging leadership in private sector networks

Certain ASNs, particularly Bharti Airtel Ltd. (AS24560), are demonstrating a positive trend toward secure validation, outperforming the national average by nearly 70%. This indicates that the technical capability for DNSSEC validation exists within the domestic market and can be scaled if adopted by other Tier-1 operators.

3. Where India could improve

The primary weaknesses in India’s DNS resilience are ranked by the lack of secure validation in high-traffic networks, the low adoption of IPv6 for DNS transport, and the high dependency on foreign TLD infrastructure.

3.1 Fragmented DNSSEC validation across major ISPs

The national validation average is suppressed by low adoption in high-market-share networks. Jio Platforms Ltd (AS55836) and National Internet Backbone (AS9829) collectively account for nearly 30% of query share but maintain validation rates below 10%. This creates a massive surface area for DNS-based attacks on the general population.

3.2 Stagnant IPv6 transition at the user edge

Despite significant national focus on IPv6, only 10.32% of observed DNS queries use IPv6 transport. This suggests that while core networks may be dual-stacked, the recursive resolution path remains predominantly IPv4, limiting the benefits of more modern, diverse routing paths available via IPv6.

6.2 Loopholes to avoid creating

  • Mandating DNSSEC signing for domains without providing easy-to-use tooling for small business registrants.
  • Mandating resolver validation without verifying that ISP support desks can troubleshoot DNSSEC-related SERVFAIL errors.
  • Focusing solely on .in security while ignoring that 67.46% of traffic is .com, which requires resolver-side validation.

Conclusion

India’s DNS resilience is currently hampered by a significant disconnect between its robust physical network presence and its outdated security configurations. The fact that only 10.12% of DNS queries are secured by DNSSEC in one of the world’s largest digital economies represents a critical vulnerability. However, the high resolution success rate (85.63%) proves that the underlying infrastructure is reliable, providing a stable foundation for security upgrades.

The proposed action plan prioritizes configuration-based "quick wins" over capital-intensive projects. By simply enabling DNSSEC validation on the recursive resolvers of the country’s largest ISPs—Jio and BSNL—India could triple its secure validation rate within months. Sequencing these low-cost administrative changes before embarking on long-term CPE modernization will build regulatory momentum and significantly harden the nation's Internet resilience against evolving cyber threats.

Measurement caveats

Data regarding authoritative nameservers for the .in ccTLD reflects only the infrastructure captured during the current scan cycle. The DNSSEC validation rates are based on sample telemetry from recursive resolvers and may vary slightly depending on user distribution and query volumes at the time of measurement. Figures for "0 authoritative nameservers" indicate a lack of visible records in the primary assessment scan and should be verified against registry-level data.
